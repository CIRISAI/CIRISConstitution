# `formal/` — the life of a mesh, and the proofs that it holds together

Start here. Everything in this directory is a chapter of one story, told in order in [`mesh_life/LIFECYCLE.md`](mesh_life/LIFECYCLE.md), and the story itself is a checked model that closes.

## The story in ten steps

1. **Three people sit down** and perform the genesis ceremony. Out comes the bundle, three seated holders, and the first canonical node, owned by nobody yet.
2. **The canonical gets an owner**, an announced person, and only then does it serve. Nothing below needs it.
3. **A person arrives**: a key, a claimed device, an announcement. Now strangers can find them.
4. **Two people become contacts.** The request crosses a forwarder sealed; after the acceptance they go direct, and the forwarder holds nothing about them.
5. **A room is founded.** Members propose, the proposed accept or decline, members resign, the others remove; an empty room dissolves.
6. **Members write.** A row is written, withdrawn, purged, and never comes back.
7. **A person consents** to their traces being captured, and revokes.
8. **An agent is bonded** to a steward, handed over, frozen, retired. One way only.
9. **The holders halt.** One latches, a lone latch lapses, a majority confirms and resumes.
10. **The mesh winds down.** A majority decides; nothing new is born; everything alive runs to its own end; the mesh has ended.

Every thing that appears in those steps has its own short story in the same voice, with its states, what moves it, how it ends, and which model proves it: [`STORIES.md`](STORIES.md).

## One model of the whole story

[`mesh_life/MeshLife.tla`](mesh_life/) has one action per sentence above and closes: three holders, two people with a device each, one room, two rows, one agent. Every path from the ceremony reaches the end, nobody is ever left owning nothing or owned by no one, a confirmed halt was always a majority, and once the holders decide to wind down the mesh always ends. Three twins loosen one rule each and fail. The numbers are in its [README](mesh_life/README.md).

## The kernel

Every chapter below is a fold over held rows and a list of transitions, and [`event_lifecycle/`](event_lifecycle/) is the one that says so in general: a node's state is the rows it holds, every verdict is a fold over them, one step appends, and the queue a person sees, the preview of an act and the door that admits it are one function (`door_is_queue`, `preview_is_execution`, `refused_previews_nothing`). The state space is finite (five states per object, closed role and policy vocabularies) over an unbounded forward-only log, which is why the story above can be checked to its end rather than sampled, and why a node, a preview and a model checker compute the same answer. CC 4.5.13, "The state processor".

## The cascade, simply

Each line below is a step the one above forces. Nothing in a lower step may say more than the step above it allows, and every step names where it lives.

1. **Ethics.** Meta-Goal M-1 ([CC 1.1](../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)), the principled decision algorithm and its order of principles with the most vulnerable party as the tie-break ([CC 1.3](../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)), the Recursive Golden Rule that binds every principal including the steward and CIRIS L3C ([CC 1.13.2](../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)), the fail-secure posture that keeps the halt in human hands ([CC 1.5](../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)), and deferral to a named human when the agent is unsure ([CC 1.9](../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)).
2. **Promises.** Eleven promises written once for human and AI participants alike, each paired with the power that could break it and the check on that power: [`constitution/digest/00_guarantees.md`](../constitution/digest/00_guarantees.md) (the G rows), the plain rules for a user and the digest for an operator beside it. Every promise line carries a marker stamped from data: works today, being built with its ticket, a rule with no code yet, or modelled.
3. **Rules.** The decimal sections of Parts 2 to 8: what a row is, who may sign it, what a substrate refuses and with which token. A promise that no rule carries is incomplete; a rule that no promise needs is suspect.
4. **Invariants.** Each rule that can be stated as something that never happens, or always eventually happens, is written as a property: 221 of them today, across 16 models, in [`INVARIANTS.tsv`](INVARIANTS.tsv), each with its CC decimal, its claim id and its verdict, and each restated over one shared vocabulary.
5. **The state table.** That vocabulary: [`STATE_TABLE.tsv`](STATE_TABLE.tsv) names the states a mesh node and the things around it can be in (242 states over 81 variables in 12 planes), where the text defines each, which row enters it, who signs, and which model realises it. A state the text defines and no model constrains is listed as such, because the gap is the point.
6. **Plane models.** One checked model per plane: the standing verdict, the accord halt and its roster arithmetic, the keys under one self, the mesh of blobs and cohorts and roots, delegation and licences and grants, the participant guarantees, erasure. Each has a configuration that closes and must-fail twins, each twin named after the wrong reading it encodes, so a reader sees what the rule prevents and not only that it holds. Where the text admitted two readings, both were run and the counterexample for the rejected one is shown.
7. **The spine.** [`cewp_node/CewpNode.tla`](cewp_node/CewpNode.tla) composes the planes over the state table and checks only what no plane sees alone: the halt is not a trust leg, an evicted key's authority ends, replication follows consent, a local row never leaves, leaving is one act. Its breaker twin encodes a trust verdict that folds the halt latch, the one cross-plane defect the spine exists to catch, and fails as it must.
8. **The catalogue.** The CSD/4 topology catalogue (twenty entries, `T0`–`T19`, vendored under [`cewp_node/catalog/`](cewp_node/catalog/)) describes the meshes the harness stands up on real nodes. [`tools/topology_to_cfg.py`](../tools/topology_to_cfg.py) turns one entry into every plane model's constants and a report of which promised states a model constrains; the same entry drives the harness, whose row export is what a trace check replays. The vocabulary of states the catalogue speaks is the constitution's ([`manifests/topology_vocabulary.json`](../manifests/topology_vocabulary.json)), so a state nobody has written down cannot be claimed.
9. **The register.** [`constitution/claims.tsv`](../constitution/claims.tsv): every load-bearing claim, established only when a pinned manifest of the owning repository publishes the named symbol, staged on a ticket until then, normative when it is a rule with no code. The markers of step 2 are stamped from it. The checker runs on every commit.

Read downward to see how a principle becomes a thing a machine refuses. Read upward to see which principle a refusal serves.

## What a model proves, and what it does not

A model-checker run says the machine as written has no bad reachable corner within the bound. A harness run says one implementation followed one path of that machine correctly. The register says which claims rest on which. None of them says the rule is wise: the record preserves evidence, the observation is bounded by the instrument, the interpretation gives it meaning, and a named party answers for the interpretation. The models here are the record and the observation. The meaning, and the responsibility for it, stay with the people who wrote the rules and run the nodes.

Every model states its residuals in its README: what it does not cover, the bounds it found, the readings it rejected. A verdict without its residuals is not a verdict.

## What a model proves, and what it does not

A model-checker run says the machine as written has no bad reachable corner within the bound. A harness run says one implementation followed one path of that machine correctly. The register says which claims rest on which. None of them says the text is right; [`ADEQUACY.md`](ADEQUACY.md) is where the claim of adequacy is decomposed into sub-claims, each with its witness and its status, and every model states its residuals in its README: what it does not cover, the bounds it found, the readings it rejected. A verdict without its residuals is not a verdict.

## Running

TLC: `java -cp tla2tools.jar tlc2.TLC -deadlock -config <cfg> <module>.tla` in the model's directory; Lean: `lake build` in the model's directory; the delegation model: `python3 authority_tree.py`. Every configuration runs in CI (`.github/workflows/formal.yml`), the closing ones to closure and each twin to its named failure. `tools/check_formal_tables.py` keeps the two tables honest against the sources, the toc and the register.
