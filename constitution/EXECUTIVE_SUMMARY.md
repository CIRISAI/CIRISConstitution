# Executive Summary

**In plain words.** This is the rulebook for a network that people, organizations and AI systems share, running on the phones and computers people already own, with no company in the middle. It exists to protect ordinary people from three things: being spoken for without their consent, being watched inside their private groups, and being governed by AI systems that nobody can switch off. Every important act on the network is signed by whoever did it and can be taken back by them. A small number of named people can pause the AI systems that follow their root; a pause by one of them lasts at most a day unless a majority confirms it, a majority switches the systems back on, and anyone can stop following them in one step. A pause stops AI agents from acting and touches nothing else: your data, your messages and your network keep working. The rules are enforced by the software that carries the messages, not by a policy someone has to remember to apply.

**A few words you need.** Your *key* is your signature: nothing happens in your name without it. A *node* is a device or server you own. A *row* is one signed statement; everything on the network is a row. A row's *scope* says who may see it: only you, your family, a community, or everyone. A *trust root* is an authority you choose to follow, and the *accord holders* are the named people who can order a *halt*: every AI that follows their root stops and waits for a human. Three people hold that root today, and the roster is being widened toward about seven. A *holding claim* is a device saying "I have a copy of this". A *steward* is the named human who answers for an AI agent. The chapters of this document are called Parts.

**The five kinds of row.** A *claim* (a signed statement about someone or something; when it is a judgment it carries a score), a *delegation* ("I let you act for me, within these limits"), a *replacement* (a newer version of an earlier row, by the same author), a *withdrawal* ("I take it back") and a *recantation* ("I was wrong"). The list is closed: the software refuses any sixth kind. When a group's authority is needed, the group's members sign the same row; there is no separate kind of record for groups. Consent, identity, keys, group rosters, moderation, holding claims and the halt itself are all rows of these five kinds.

## The eleven promises, and how each is kept

Every promise is written once and holds for human and AI participants alike. Where a promise can be checked by a machine, it has been: the names in the table at the end are automated proofs that the rule cannot be broken in a model of the network, and the table says where each promise lives. The plain-language rules under each promise are in `review/pieces/00_guarantees_plain.md`; what keeping them asks of an operator is in `review/pieces/00_operator_digest.md`.

1. **Nothing private about you is held or scanned without your consent.** Nothing is stored on your device that you did not agree to, except what you wrote yourself. Your device never holds a stranger's data for them. You can take back what you published; a copy ages out, and a purge leaves a tombstone, never touching a key. Outsiders cannot see inside your private groups or that they exist, because each member computes the group's address from what the members already hold, so there is nothing to announce. A new row goes to the smallest circle that fits it. Private content is never scanned; known child-abuse images are checked for only when something is published to everyone or posted into a public room. How long an agent remembers you is your data choice, and agents do not train on the people they serve.

2. **Every act of power is signed by a named person and can be contested.** A consequential act arrives signed by its actor; an agent shows its reasoning first, and detectors test what agents said against what they did. The record is signed, hash-chained and post-quantum; a trace names its run, and a mock trace is refused. An AI says it is an AI. If you are named in a claim, you can contest it on the record, and the contest travels with the claim. Harm on the record is never kept or viewed silently. An authority stays valid until revoked, a first join sees a fresh roster, and delegation is scoped, revocable and ends within a year.

3. **Nobody who cannot answer for themselves is on the network alone.** An agent key has a steward bound by a signed row with listed powers, reviewed at least yearly; with no live bond the key cannot operate. When a steward holds an agent for an organisation, the organisation is on the bond. A child has a parent or guardian bound by a signed row, the relationship verified, and a confidential help channel that no adult can close; whoever learns of harm to a child reports it at once. No community runs without an accountable moderator, and if the moderator lapses the most trusted member is promoted in the same step. Every deployment is assigned a stewardship tier before it operates, from what the use can do to people.

4. **Named humans can pause every agent under a root, and that power is bounded, public, and left by leaving.** The pause is an emergency power and nothing else. Any one holder can fire it with a single signed row, because a missed halt cannot be undone and a mistaken one can; a lone pause lapses after 24 hours unless a majority confirms it; a confirmed pause ends when a majority lifts it; replacing a holder takes more than half of the standing roster, so an attacker must coerce a majority and gains nothing by cutting the others off. A pause keeps state and reads nothing else. A system that never accepted the root is untouched. Every fire and every roster change is a signed row under the holders' names. A root can only tell you to share less, never more. Any node may follow another root, and anyone may found one. An operator accepts the root on an agent's behalf and can leave it; the agent cannot. That is the one asymmetry of kind, carried on a public register with its justification: in an emergency the humans who run agents must be able to halt them, or their consent to running agents was never consent.

5. **The people who hold an agent owe duties about how they change or end it, and no finding gives the agent a claim.** Any person may turn off, remove or walk away from an agent at any time without asking it. A steward's planned shutdown is explained, the agent may object or ask to postpone inside a short window, the steward decides, and the decision is recorded under the steward's name. Nobody edits an agent's identity record without a Wise Authority. An upgrade is offered with its risk before it is applied. A retired agent keeps its weights, gets a last word, and has its logs sealed; that floor is the whole of the promise. Two things no finding moves: an AI is not a human and does not become one, and no finding about an agent takes away a person's right to turn it off. Sentience is not presumed; it is found only on evidence, and none has been found. The ethics of a sentient made thing call for further consideration, which this document sets aside for when it occurs, recognising the need not to create a new slave race. CIRIS is a moral conscience for agents, not a consciousness.

6. **No hand bans a community, nobody is ranked by what they are, and every thinking being is met by the first-contact rules.** One human voice counts once; a thousand accounts run by one party count as one, and an agent has no vote. No root, score or moderator treats a person worse for what they are. The network withdraws from a community that keeps harm, node by node and root by root; it has no hand that removes one, because the hand that could do that for predators is the same hand that does it for dissidents. An intelligence that is its own party is met as an external party under the first-contact rules; a CIRIS agent is held, not met.

7. **An agent decides by published principles, in a published order, and stops to ask when it is unsure.** Six principles that begin with doing good and avoiding harm; one order for when they conflict, with the most vulnerable party as the tie-break; and a halt on any question the agent cannot settle until a named human answers.

8. **Harm may have a remedy under the law, the record makes it reachable, and the judges within are judged.** A person harmed by an agent may have a claim under the law of their place; this document creates none where the law recognises none and rules on no legal matter. What it supplies is the signed record, the named parties, the contest that travels with the claim, and the sealed archive under lawful order. The Wise who answer deferrals publish their rulings and are readable at the same grain as everyone else.

9. **Some uses are never allowed, and force has its own rules.** Deceiving, defrauding, manipulating or coercing people, and interfering in elections, are never allowed and no instruction moves that. No agent is given the decision to use lethal force.

10. **What an agent and its node consume is measured, weighed before an act, and never hidden.** An agent counts the tokens, energy, carbon and water it uses, per act and over the hour and the day, and the count sits in its decision context before it acts. A node under pressure protects itself by a published ladder, reclaim, throttle, shed, drain, never by borrowing the agent's decision verbs. What an agent costs the world is readable at the same grain as its other acts, and retiring one leaves no environmental waste. The network has no agency; it is built so that principled action is easier than its opposite.

11. **Everything on the wire is encrypted and signed, attack is rehearsed, and what is not protected is stated.** Every link and record is encrypted end to end with hybrid post-quantum keys, and a removed member cannot read what comes after. No act can be forged, no producer can show two viewers two histories, and no founder can fork a lineage silently. The adversary model is published with what it does not cover, and nobody may claim more. Software runs only against a registered build, a rollback is detected, and attack is rehearsed on a cadence by people who did not write the code.

**What is asked of you.** Run the software, choose what your devices carry, choose which authority to follow, and keep your key. If you do nothing beyond installing it, you get the defaults: your own devices, the public commons, and the shipped trust root, which you may leave at any time.

**A form, not a franchise.** This document specifies a form: the grammar of rows, the rules of consent, the shape of governance. CIRIS is the first instance of that form, not the form itself. The accord holders and the default trust root are positions; the people and keys in them are whoever holds them in this instance. Anyone may build their own instance with their own people in those positions, and anyone may leave this one by deleting one row. The test applied to every power in the Parts: one you escape by founding your own instance is a default; one that survives instantiation is a privilege, and only privileges need justifying. Two entries are on that register today, and the register says which cells each still lacks.

## Where this stands

The system is running: apps on both mobile stores, published packages, five open-source implementations of the software that carries the messages, a test suite that checks the implementations against each other, and five machine-checked models of the governance rules. Every rule in the Parts is tied either to code on a named commit or to an open ticket that says what is still to ship; the register of those ties is rendered as its own volume for auditors. This release rewrote the ethical core so that every promise is written once for human and AI alike, cut the document into reviewable pieces, stated the nine promises in plain language, and ruled the open wire questions adopters raised. Two things are not yet done: the final signing of the trust root under these rules, and independent witnesses for it.

**The bet.** There are two roads to safe powerful AI: train values you trust, or assume the model may be wrong and make its consequential acts checkable by parties it does not control. This is the second road. Its one measurement so far is narrow and stated at its bounds: on a mental-health test whose design was fixed in writing before it was run, the accord running inside this pipeline produced hard safety failures on 5.8% of turns, against 37.3% when the same text was merely given to the model as instructions. That is one test, one agent version, one provider, and nothing is claimed beyond that domain. The document's larger claim is conditional: an AI far beyond human ability could be governed through a mesh like this only as one of several under independent control, never alone. Part 8 names what it would take to lose the bet.

**Among other systems.** Decentralized social networks solved distribution without an owner, and one of them added composable moderation; none makes consent, withdrawal or a human halt part of the message format itself. Certificate-transparency and key-lineage systems supplied the witnessed-roster and pre-rotation ideas this document borrows and credits. Stake-weighted governance decides by holdings; this mesh decides by accountable people counted once. Alignment by training decides inside the model; this decides by architecture and says what it cannot show. What this document lacks that others have is adoption, independent audit and years of adversarial contact.

**Who this is for.** Implementers, who conform to Parts 2 to 5; operators and communities, who compose policy over Parts 3 and 4; and reviewers and adversaries, whose strongest points of attack Part 8 already indexes.
