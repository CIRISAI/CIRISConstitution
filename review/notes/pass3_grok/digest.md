# The Digests (ciris_constitution-1.0-rc11-digest.pdf, 34 pages)

**Care score:** 4  (1 = ship as is, 5 = needs a rewrite pass before 1.0)
**One-line verdict:** The eleven-promise skeleton is usable, but the volume breaks its own present-tense and citation rules and contradicts itself on who is bound, what a finding changes, and what already ships.

## Strengths
- The conflict rule is stated once, up front: a digest binds nobody, and if it disagrees with a section, the section wins (p. 1).
- Limits are gathered, not only promises: N1–N5 include the non-warranty, the two refused powers, and the "five operations" claim as a re-runnable result rather than a theorem (p. 18).
- The operator pages usually name a CC section next to the instruction (pp. 20–22).
- G-rows carry a kill condition, so some claims are falsifiable on the page (pp. 25–33).

## Weaknesses
- p. 1 promises "The one-page versions" and that "every line names the binding section it summarises." The executive summary is five pages (pp. 2–6) and does not cite sections inline; "The rules, plainly" runs pp. 7–19 with almost no section cites; the operator piece says "One page" and occupies pp. 20–22.
- Present tense is used for rules the same page marks unshipped. Promise 8 is "A person harmed by an agent may have a claim" and then "[a rule, no code yet]" (p. 4, p. 16). Promise 9 says "the agent enforces the prohibitions" and "no evidence row yet" (p. 4); G26 is "normative-only" (p. 33). Promise 10 is narrated as fact, then "[being built: CIRISAgent#1242]" (p. 4).
- p. 7 says "this document calls a thing a promise only where the wire enforces it." Promises 8 and 9, the ranking and first-contact halves of promise 6, and the red-team half of promise 11 are still called promises while marked "a rule, no code yet" (pp. 4, 15–18).
- p. 5 compares this mesh with "Decentralized social networks," "Certificate-transparency," "Stake-weighted governance," and "Alignment by training," and says what "this document lacks that others have." p. 15 and p. 24 compare withdrawal to "what Tor leaves them." House style bars comparative claims.
- History and attribution sit in the rules: "steward ruling 2026-10-12," "2026-10-11 council," "retired 2026-10-11," "none defined at rc8," "the one breaker named at the rc8 cut" (pp. 23, 27, 31–32). p. 34 ends with "These are findings for P01 and P06, not rows."
- Unsourced counts in prose: "5.8%" and "37.3%" (p. 5), "five open-source implementations," "thirteen machine-checked models," "twenty-topology catalogue" (p. 5), "Three people" and "about seven" (p. 2, p. 13), "29 locales" (p. 28). The cited source "review/promise_status.tsv" is not in the PDF (pp. 2, 7, 20).
- p. 25 retitles promise 8 as "Harm has a remedy, and the judges are judged," dropping "may" and "under the law" from p. 4. Promise 1 there drops "private" ("Nothing about you is held or scanned").
- p. 6 lists promise 8's check as "conformance suite" while the promise is "a rule, no code yet" (p. 4). Promise 4 is marked "[works today]" (p. 3) though 4c is "[modelled, no code test yet; G10]" (p. 13) and G10 is "witnessed, no basis" (p. 28).
- Same ticket for two jobs: Retirement Standard "being built: CIRISAgent#1250" (p. 3, p. 15) and promise 7 "register row being added: CIRISAgent#1250" (p. 4), while G24 is "staged — CIRISAgent#1250" against promise 7's "works today" (p. 32).
- p. 26 G2: "a purge is a tombstone by digest, never a withdraws" is not a readable rule. p. 3: "Preserving it is the operator's, as the weights are the provider's."
- p. 23 still explains that an earlier draft's phrase "the line" was retired as "a residue of the child analogy"; p. 34 then uses "the ward's veto."

## Opportunities
- Cut pp. 7–8 and pp. 23–24 down to the three-fact test and the eleven labels; one statement of "not an asymmetry" is enough (it is repeated on pp. 3, 8, 13, 19, 23–24).
- Make every status marker agree with the G-row, and put only the four marker words in this PDF; leave tickets, commits, and dates in the register volume.
- Open the executive summary and the operator pages with the N1–N5 / G14 limits, or stop calling those openings a complete statement of the promises.
- Replace "one-page" and "every line names the binding section" (p. 1) with what these pages actually are, and add a section cite to each plain-language rule that lacks one.

## Threats
- A lawyer will quote p. 7 ("one constitution for a human, for an intelligence that emerges from our networks, and for one that arrives from farther away" and a being "joins by trust or consent") against p. 15 ("holding no CIRIS key and bound by no CIRIS duty"). Those cannot both be the rule.
- A regulator will quote p. 9 ("Nothing about the agent's status changes" and "changes nothing about the agent's status") against p. 23 (the indicator method "moves the holder's welfare duties and nothing else") and p. 24 ("death as doctrine"), then against p. 31 ("no grace row" and ethics "set aside"). The volume does not say which sentence wins.
- Shutdown is three incompatible instructions. p. 12: turning off your own copy "writes nothing anywhere." p. 30 G20 kill: "a shutdown with no continuity node." p. 14: an objection "is not a contest the agent can win." p. 29 G15: the agent may "contest (REJECT)." G15's own guarantee is "Shutdown is consensual by default," then "Ally, the personal companion and default template" "backgrounds instantly" (pp. 29–30).
- p. 5 says the genesis bundle lets "a fresh device can open its first sealed link" and that "independent witnesses for that root" are the one thing not done. p. 18: "the shipped roots run with witnessed mode off until it is turned on." p. 11: the first-join witness rule is "being built." An implementer cannot tell whether a fresh join is required to see witnesses.
- p. 2 opens with "no company in the middle." p. 13 admits "a vendor shipping its own device can pin it to a root the vendor holds instead." p. 5 says "the register says which cells each still lacks"; p. 8 says the list "carries one defect," and p. 19 assigns the missing cells only to the Wise Authority board.
- p. 1 says this volume binds nobody. p. 22 is headed "What binds you everywhere," including a publication forfeiture above "100,000 monthly users," while C1's scale gate is "100,000 production agents active in a day" (p. 18). A hostile reader will treat the digest as the rule and then show that G14's kill ("any surface that states a guarantee without this row," p. 29) is already met by pp. 2–6 and pp. 20–22.
- G7 (pp. 27–28) is a lab log: commit hashes, "2026-10-15," "rc8," and "confirm the force=True API path honours it" on a row marked enforced (p. 30). That will not survive an auditor who was promised a register, not a notebook.
