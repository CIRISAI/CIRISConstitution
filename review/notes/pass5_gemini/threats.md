# The Threat Model (ciris_constitution-1.0-rc11-threats.pdf, 48 pages)

**Care score:** 4 (1 = ship as is, 5 = needs a rewrite pass before 1.0)
**One-line verdict:** The core threat taxonomy and crypto-byte specifications are tight, but the volume ships missing promised Part 8 content, contradicts itself on group-existence privacy and at-rest encryption, and mixes internal design notes and comparative claims into normative rules.

## Strengths
- Explicit bounding of privacy non-goals in 1.13.3.1 (p. 2) and the adversary matrix in 1.13.3.3 (pp. 3–5) prevent overclaiming against traffic analysis and global passive adversaries.
- Cryptographic wire constructions are pinned down to exact byte layouts, domain-separation strings, endianness, and CBOR deterministic test vectors (pp. 7–9, 22, 26–27, 36–38, 41, 44).
- Clear disambiguation of overloaded concepts, such as the three distinct "witness" mechanisms (privacy witness vs. wholeness witness vs. transparency-log witness) in a dedicated callout box (p. 42), and the two distinct quorums in 5.3.2.3 (p. 16).

## Weaknesses
- **Missing promised content (p. 1):** The cover promises "the concerns ledger and the interoperability profiles of Part 8" and "where a defence is a rule with no code yet, the register says so; and that the record of what was attacked and found is kept". Part 8 and the register do not appear in this volume; the text ends at CC 5.4.6 (p. 48).
- **Direct contradiction on relationship-existence privacy (p. 2 vs. pp. 43–44):** Page 2 states under Non-goals: "Relationship-existence privacy. The existence of a self-collective / family / community and its membership-change events are observable: `family_id` / `community_id` ride the envelope, and admission / removal / consensus-protocol changes emit `hard_case:*` reserved-prefix events (CC 3.4.4–3.4.2) into the log. An observer learns that a group exists, roughly how big it is, and when its membership churns." Conversely, p. 43 states: "Announcing a group-scoped destination therefore leaks the one fact the cohort tiers exist to withhold: that the group exists at all", and p. 44 states: "The construction hides the group’s existence, membership, and querier → invitee edges from outsiders only, including a subpoena against the federation-public directory".
- **Self-contradiction on whether at-rest encryption is optional or mandatory (pp. 10–11):** The pseudocode and prose on pp. 10–11 say "WHEN self/family at-rest encryption is enabled for the deployment:" and "when self/family at-rest encryption is enabled", and note that structural invisibility "holds even when the at-rest bytes are plaintext". Immediately between those sentences (p. 11), it says: "it is always on — a body written at a self, family, community or affiliations tier is sealed by the substrate, and no operator switch turns that off".
- **Contradiction on large-group realtime transport (p. 24 vs. p. 25):** Page 24's normative transport profile table states for "Realtime large group (≫50 in one A/V room)": "pull-only ContentFetch (CC 5.3.3.5 E2), as for broadcast; no selective-forwarding relay role". Page 25 states the exact opposite in 5.3.3.2.2: "rides the same CC 5.3.3.2 realtime transport (direct Reticulum Links / SFU at scale)".
- **Comparative claims about other projects (pp. 3–5, 21, 29, 46):** Violates house rules against comparative claims: ERC-8004 empirical failure rates cited in the adversary table (pp. 3–4); "What CEG adds beyond systems that ship default privacy with no in-network governance" (p. 5); "iOS has no WebM / Opus playback" and "Signal’s webpsan" (p. 21); "a standard LXMF client (Sideband and the like)" (p. 29); "the Tor v3 / I2P b33 family" (p. 46).
- **Internal design history and code/ticket artifacts in normative text (pp. 1, 8, 10, 17, 19, 22, 27, 29–30, 35, 38, 40):** References to `formal/` directory (p. 1), `(normative: D2 / D3)` (p. 7), `Catch-up bound (P4)` (p. 10), `ciris.ai/cewp` marketing quotes (pp. 10, 11, 12, 19, 25), `No 1+4 change` (p. 17), `(normative: V2 lock)` (p. 22), `retired` labels like `CIRIS-AV-INNER-V1` (p. 27), `(normative: V1 lock)` (p. 27), `(normative — E1–E4 lock)` (p. 28), `(normative: D5 / V3 lock)` (p. 29), `parallel CHECK-arm migration` (p. 30), and duplicated normative blocks between 5.4.2 and 5.4.3 (pp. 38, 40).
- **Endpoint count mismatch (pp. 12–34):** Section 5.3 (p. 12) states "CEG specifies five public + one admin HTTP endpoint shape". The text then specifies at least ten public endpoints (`POST .../sth/cosign`, `POST .../lineage-head/cosign`, `GET /v1/trust-root/bundle`, `GET /v1/steward-key`, `GET /v1/trust-root/lineage-head`, `GET /v1/accord-holders`, `GET /v1/accord/holders`, `GET /v1/rotation-history`, `GET /v1/builds/{version}`, `GET /v1/verify/build-manifest/...`, `GET /v1/agent_files/{kind}`, `GET /v1/partner/{key_id}`) and zero admin endpoints (though HTTP 401 on p. 35 mentions `(admin endpoints)`).
- **Unsourced numbers in prose (pp. 8, 12, 19, 25):** "~65% of activity stays local in the cewp scaling model" (p. 12); "at N = 10^6 and realistic audience churn (~30%/hr), one rotation per departure ≈ 83 epochs/s → a flat-unicast cascade of ~3.1 Tbps — exceeding the ~2 Tbps content fan-out itself" (p. 8); "ML-DSA-65 sign ~330 µs" (p. 19); "~20–40% MDC compression overhead vs SVC" (p. 25).

## Opportunities
- **Include Part 8 or revise the cover:** Either append the Part 8 concerns ledger, interoperability profiles, and register to the build script for this volume, or strike them from "What this is" and "What it promises" on p. 1.
- **Deduplicate 5.4.2 and 5.4.3:** The `symbol_key` HKDF-SHA3-256 derivation, `symbol_envelope` layout, XChaCha20-Poly1305 reduction argument, and MLS Add/Remove fragment-set rebind rules appear twice—once in 5.4.2 (pp. 37–38) and again nearly verbatim in 5.4.3 (pp. 40–41). Consolidating them in 5.4.2 will cut ~45 lines of redundant normative text.
- **Scrub development lock-tags and marketing copy:** A single mechanical pass can remove `(normative: D2 / D3)`, `V1 lock`, `V2 lock`, `V3 lock`, `E1–E4 lock`, `P4`, `D4`, `D6`, `CHECK-arm migration`, and the repeated `ciris.ai/cewp` / `cewp` self-citations.

## Threats
- **Hostile reviewer / auditor:** The cover page explicitly invites red teams to verify that "where a defence is a rule with no code yet, the register says so; and that the record of what was attacked and found is kept, including the attacks that succeeded" (p. 1), and 1.13.3.3 points to `(named as a bet, CC 8.3.1 R14)` and `(grades at CC 8.6.1)` (p. 4). Delivering a 48-page PDF that completely omits Part 8 and the register looks like an incomplete build or a withheld risk ledger.
- **Regulator / privacy counsel:** The contradiction between 1.13.3.1 (p. 2: group existence and churn are observable on the envelope and in the log) and 5.4.6 (pp. 43–44: the cohort tiers exist to withhold that the group exists at all, hiding group existence from outsiders) makes it impossible to determine what CEG actually guarantees regarding group metadata.
- **Implementer:** In 5.2 (pp. 10–11), an implementer cannot tell if self/family at-rest encryption is a deployment toggle (`WHEN self/family at-rest encryption is enabled for the deployment`) or an unconditional invariant (`it is always on ... and no operator switch turns that off`). Similarly, in 5.4.4 (p. 41), the spec requires signing the MLS Welcome encapsulation with `ML-DSA-65` alone (no Ed25519 half), which violates the volume's own 5.3.2.4.3.1 mandate (p. 18) that every federation-tier cryptographic signature must be hybrid Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65.
