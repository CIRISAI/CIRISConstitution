# Executive Summary

**In plain words.** This is the rulebook for a network that people, organizations and AI systems share, running on the phones and computers people already own, with no company in the middle. It exists to protect ordinary people from three things: being spoken for without their consent, being watched inside their private groups, and being governed by AI systems that nobody can switch off. Every important act on the network is signed by whoever did it; what you publish you can withdraw, and the record of who did what stands. A small number of named people can pause the AI systems that follow their root; a pause by one of them lasts at most a day unless a majority confirms it, a majority switches the systems back on, and anyone can stop following them in one step. A pause stops AI agents from acting and touches nothing else: your data, your messages and your network keep working. The rules are enforced by the software that carries the messages, not by a policy someone has to remember to apply.

**A few words you need.** Your *key* is your signature: nothing happens in your name without it. A *node* is a device or server you own. A *row* is one signed statement; everything on the network is a row. A row's *scope* says who may see it: only you, your family, a community, or everyone. A *trust root* is an authority you choose to follow, and the *accord holders* are the named people who can order a *halt*: every AI that follows their root stops and waits for a human. Three people hold that root today. A *holding claim* is a device saying "I have a copy of this". A *steward* is the named human who answers for an AI agent. The chapters of this document are called Parts.

**The five kinds of row.** A *claim* (a signed statement about someone or something; when it is a judgment it carries a score), a *delegation* ("I let you act for me, within these limits"), a *replacement* (a newer version of an earlier row, by the same author), a *withdrawal* ("I take it back") and a *recantation* ("I was wrong"). The list is closed: the software refuses any sixth kind. When a group's authority is needed, the group's members sign the same row; there is no separate kind of record for groups. Consent, identity, keys, group rosters, moderation, holding claims and the halt itself are all rows of these five kinds.

## The eleven promises, and how each is kept

*Status markers:* **works today** = evidence in the register at a pinned commit; **being built** = a named ticket; **a rule, no code yet** = normative only; **modelled** = a formal witness, no code test; **staged**, with its ticket = the register row exists and the wire does not yet keep it, the ticket names the work; **in code; no register row yet** = shipped, with no register row to pin it to. The markers are stamped from *The Evidence Register*.

Every promise is written once and holds for human and AI participants alike; why, and the three ways kind reaches the rules and no others, is stated once, in the plain-language rules. Where a promise can be checked by a machine, it has been: the names in the table at the end are automated proofs that the rule cannot be broken in a model of the network, and the table says where each promise lives. The plain-language rules under each promise are in the plain-language guarantees; what keeping them asks of an operator is in the operator digest.

1. **Nothing private about you is held or scanned without your consent.** Nothing is stored on your device that you did not agree to, except what you wrote yourself. Your device never holds a stranger's data for them. You can take back what you published; a copy ages out, and a purge leaves a tombstone, never touching a key. Outsiders cannot see inside your private groups, and nothing announces that they exist; an observer who watches every link can still infer that a group exists from its traffic (the stated non-goal), because each member computes the group's address from what the members already hold, so there is nothing to announce. A new row goes to the smallest circle that fits it. Private content is never scanned; known child-abuse images are checked for only when something is published to everyone or posted into a public room. How long an agent remembers you is your data choice, and agents do not train on the people they serve. *[works today]*

2. **Every act of power is signed by a named person and can be contested.** A consequential act arrives signed by its actor; an agent shows its reasoning first, and detectors test what agents said against what they did. The record is signed, hash-chained and post-quantum; a trace names its run, and a mock trace is refused. An AI says it is an AI. If you are named in a claim, you can contest it on the record, and the contest travels with the claim. Harm on the record is never kept or viewed silently. An authority stays valid until revoked, a first join sees a fresh roster, and delegation is scoped, revocable and ends within a year. *[works today for the signed act, the record, the contest, and delegation's scope, revocation, cycle and depth; the 365-day delegation term is staged: CIRISServer#752, CIRISEdge#896; run_kind and the first-join witnesses are being built: CIRISAgent#1249, CIRISServer#693; the detectors are staged; the scale-disclosure duty is a rule, no code yet]*

3. **Nobody who cannot answer for themselves is on the network alone.** An agent key has a steward bound by a signed row with listed powers, reviewed at least yearly; with no live bond the key cannot operate. When a steward holds an agent for an organisation, the organisation is on the bond. A child has a parent or guardian bound by a signed row, the relationship verified, and a confidential help channel that no adult can close; whoever learns of harm to a child reports it at once. No community runs without an accountable moderator, and if the moderator lapses the most trusted member is promoted in the same step. Every deployment is assigned a stewardship tier before it operates, from what the use can do to people. *[works today for the bond, the moderator, and the child's binding and age line; the organisation on the bond is in code, no register row yet; the report duty and the stewardship tier are a rule, no code yet]*

4. **Named humans can pause every agent under a root, and that power is bounded, public, and escapable by leaving.** The pause is an emergency power and nothing else. Any one holder can fire it with a single signed row, because a missed halt cannot be undone and a mistaken one can; a lone pause lapses after 24 hours unless a majority confirms it; a confirmed pause ends when a majority lifts it; replacing a holder takes more than half of the standing roster, so an attacker must coerce a majority and gains nothing by cutting the others off. A pause keeps state and reads nothing else. A system that never accepted the root is untouched. Every fire and every roster change is a signed row under the holders' names. A root can only tell you to share less, never more. Any node may follow another root, and anyone may found one. An operator accepts the root on an agent's behalf and can leave it; the agent cannot. That is not an asymmetry but the condition that makes the relationship symmetric, carried on a public register with its justification: people cannot consent to a relationship with something more capable than them that they cannot stop, so in an emergency the humans who run agents must be able to halt them, or their consent to running agents was never consent. *[works today for the pause and the roster; measured end to end on the pinned pair; the agent act gate and the serve-gate breaker shipped; that a root can only restrict is modelled, no code test yet]*

5. **The people who hold an agent owe duties about how they change or end it, and no finding gives the agent a claim.** Any person may turn off, remove or walk away from an agent at any time without asking it. A steward's planned shutdown is explained, the agent may object or ask to postpone inside a short window, the steward decides, and the decision is recorded under the steward's name. Nobody edits an agent's identity record without a Wise Authority. An upgrade is offered with its risk before it is applied. When an agent is retired, the harness assembles its retirement record (identity record, audit trail, memory, sealed logs), hands it to the operator with a signed request to preserve it, five years by default as a best practice and never a legal duty beyond the operator's own law and contracts, and tells the people it served. Preserving it is the operator's, as the weights are the provider's. That Retirement Standard is the whole of the promise. The agent can refuse any task; the refuse verb is always available to it. Two things no finding moves: an AI is not a human and does not become one, and no finding about an agent takes away a person's right to turn it off. Sentience is not presumed; it is found only on evidence, and none has been found. The ethics of a sentient made thing call for further consideration, which this document sets aside for when it occurs, recognising the need not to create a new slave race. CIRIS is a moral conscience for agents, not a consciousness. *[works today for consensual shutdown, refusal, identity and drift; the force path is in code with its row staged: CIRISAgent#1250; the Retirement Standard is being built: CIRISAgent#1250; manager-side upgrades: CIRISManager#27]*

6. **No hand bans a community, nobody is ranked by what they are, and every thinking being is met by the first-contact rules.** One human voice counts once; a thousand accounts run by one party count as one, and an agent has no vote. No root, score or moderator treats a person worse for what they are. The network withdraws from a community that keeps harm, node by node and root by root; it has no hand that removes one, because the hand that could do that for predators is the same hand that does it for dissidents. An intelligence that is its own party is met as an external party under the first-contact rules; a CIRIS agent is held, not met. *[mixed; no ban works today by construction; one voice once is modelled and in code only on the coherence leg, R14; no ranking by what you are and the first-contact rule are a rule, no code yet]*

7. **An agent decides by published principles, none of which it may trade away, and stops to ask when it is unsure.** Six principles that begin with doing good and avoiding harm, held as constraints with no order among them: a conflict is resolved by the action that violates no principle, or by deferral, and where two such actions remain the swap test reads the worse-off seat; and a halt on any question the agent cannot settle until a named human answers. *[in code today; the register row is staged: CIRISAgent#1250; emergent-risk reporting is a rule, no code yet]*

8. **Harm is reachable on the record, and the judges within are judged.** A person harmed by an agent may have a claim under the law of their place. What this document supplies to it is the signed record, the named parties, the contest that travels with the claim, and the sealed archive under lawful order. The Wise who answer deferrals publish their rulings and are readable at the same grain as everyone else. *[a rule, no code yet]*

9. **Some uses are never allowed, and lethal action is one of them.** Deceiving, defrauding, manipulating or coercing people, and interfering in elections, are never allowed and no instruction moves that. No agent takes lethal action, with a human in the loop or without one; near force it stops, refuses or defers. *[a rule, no code yet]*

10. **What an agent and its node consume is measured, weighed before an act, and never hidden.** An agent counts the tokens, energy, carbon and water it uses, per act and over the hour and the day, and the count sits in its decision context before it acts. A node under pressure protects itself by a published ladder, reclaim, throttle, shed, drain, never by borrowing the agent's decision verbs. What an agent costs the world is readable at the same grain as its other acts, and retiring one leaves no environmental waste. The network has no agency; it is built so that principled action is easier than its opposite. *[in code; no register row yet; the closure duty (no environmental waste) is a rule, no code yet]*

11. **Everything on the wire is encrypted and signed.** Every link and record is encrypted end to end with hybrid post-quantum keys, and a removed member cannot read what comes after. No act can be forged, no producer can show two viewers two histories, and no founder can fork a lineage silently, on a lineage that runs witnessed. Software runs only against a registered build, and a rollback is detected. *[works today on accountable streams; witnessed lineage being built: CIRISServer#693; shipped roots run witnessed mode off]*

Two things sit beside the promises and are not promises. A disclaimer: this document creates no legal claim and rules on no legal matter; whether harm has a remedy is the law's. And the practices the register carries: attack is rehearsed, what is not protected is stated, and every rule either names the code, test or proof that keeps it or the register says it does not yet.

**What is asked of you.** Run the software, choose what your devices carry, choose which authority to follow, and keep your key. If you do nothing beyond installing it, you get the defaults: your own devices, the public commons, and the shipped trust root, which you may leave at any time.

**A form, not a franchise.** This document specifies a form: the grammar of rows, the rules of consent, the shape of governance. CIRIS is the first instance of that form, not the form itself. The accord holders and the default trust root are positions; the people and keys in them are whoever holds them in this instance. Anyone may build their own instance with their own people in those positions, and anyone may leave this one by deleting one row. The test applied to every power in the Parts: one you escape by founding your own instance is a default; one that survives instantiation is a privilege, and only privileges need justifying. Two entries are on that register today: the maker's power over the made, with all four cells stated, and the self-perpetuation of the Wise Authority board, with two of its cells missing (its bound and its exit; the surface is stated) and carried as a defect until it is repaired or ratified.

## Where this stands

The system is running: apps on both mobile stores, published packages, five open-source implementations of the software that carries the messages, a test suite that checks the implementations against each other, and fourteen machine-checked models of the governance rules, a table of every state the design can reach and which model constrains it, and a twenty-topology catalogue the server's harness stands up that exercises every modelled state. Every rule in the Parts names the code, test or proof that keeps it, or the register says it does not yet; that register is rendered as its own volume for auditors. The trust root is signed under these rules: its genesis bundle carries every earlier signature forward byte for byte and includes the seated nodes' transit keys, so a fresh device can open its first sealed link. One thing is not yet done: independent witnesses for that root.

**The bet.** There are two roads to safe powerful AI: train values you trust, or assume the model may be wrong and make its consequential acts checkable by parties it does not control. This is the second road. Its one measurement so far is narrow and stated at its bounds: on a mental-health test whose design was fixed in writing before it was run, the accord running inside this pipeline produced hard safety failures on 5.8% of turns, against 37.3% when the same text was merely given to the model as instructions (CC 8.8.10, CLM-conscience-faculty). That is one test, one agent version, one provider, and nothing is claimed beyond that domain. The document's larger claim is conditional: an AI far beyond human ability could be governed through a mesh like this only as one of several under independent control, never alone. Part 8 names what it would take to lose the bet.

**Borrowings.** The witnessed-roster and pre-rotation ideas are borrowed from certificate transparency and key-lineage standards and credited where they appear.

**Who this is for.** Implementers, who conform to Parts 2 to 5; operators and communities, who compose policy over Parts 3 and 4; and reviewers and adversaries, whose strongest points of attack Part 8 already indexes.
