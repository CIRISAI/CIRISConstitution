The three that matter most:

1. **`need:presence` beyond a leaf** (26). The new `leaf` part settles presence by segment value. What is left is presence by another member's value, by which instance the row is, one-of, and must-not. `trust:{job}:{version}` shows the gap: the cell holds 2 of the 12 members the table states for a charter or an acceptance, because the other ten are conditioned on "a witnessed lineage", "the accord's charter", "a charter recovery" or "a delegates_to bearing trust:accepts:v1", none of which the generator can read as a leaf. A `when` part (a predicate over the row's own members) would take most of these.
2. **`need:equal` and `need:relation` together** (42 rows, 33 statements): a member tied to another member, to a column beside the row, or to a row of a named family. These are what admission actually checks on membership, census, identity:occurrence and deferral, and the body cell cannot say any of them. One part, `constraints: [{lhs, op, rhs}]` with `rhs` allowed to be `ref(<member>).<column>`, would hold both.
3. **`need:bound` where the cell already carries the member** (19): the cell says `uint` and the prose says 3, 4, or 0 / 1. The value is in the signed-object table's note cell, not its type cell, so the generated cell is weaker than the text for `version`, `window_periods` and `share_index` on eight families. Cheapest to fix: tighten the type in `constitution/signed_object.tsv`.
