# Why and when a family leaves the default row

Generated by `tools/build_family_model.py` from `formal/derivation/departure_reasons.json`; do not edit.

Unless a reason below applies, a family's rows are placed by their signer at any scope, may name anyone, are signed by one key, are judged when they arrive, may be withdrawn by their signer or by a party they name, travel to their audience, are read from the common table, may be removed on the trust root's cosigned notice, and are bound by no policy row. A family leaves that row only for a reason listed here. Each reason says when it applies and why, rests on words that stand in the Constitution, and names the cells it moves. The build refuses a departure with no reason and a reason whose cell has returned to the default; a cell with no reason yet is listed open with the question that would give it one.

## The family is not built

**When.** The store holds no rows of the family: the census read no cell for it, or the family is held shut.

**Why.** Its cells are unread, not different. When the family is built each cell sits on the default or takes a reason of its own.

**Basis.** The family is not built; the census read nothing.

## The family is a key

**When.** The family's rows are a key, a device standing under an identity, or the revocation of either.

**Why.** Every other row is checked against these. They are minted on the key plane and travel as far as any row they verify, they are read from their own tables, and a purge of an author's rows leaves the key held, so what it signed can still be checked.

**Text.** CC 2.4: "a row is admitted only once its signer's key-claim row is held"

## The act is a body's, not one signer's

**When.** The row changes what a body is or binds its members, so the family names the protocol its cosigners must meet.

**Why.** One signer cannot carry a quorum's act, and neither one signer nor one subject may undo one: a cosigned row is withdrawn only by a withdrawal cosigned under the same protocol.

**Text.** CC 2.4: "A single-signer row cannot carry a quorum's act"

## The party named must not be able to remove it

**When.** The row is another party's measurement, licence, revocation, takedown notice or accountability record about its subject.

**Why.** A subject that could withdraw adverse evidence about itself would launder its own record. Its signer may withdraw it; the subject's path is reconsideration, on which it holds standing.

**Text.** CC 2.4.1.1: "A party that could retire the adverse rows about itself and keep the rest would launder its reputation"

## The record is nobody's to purge

**When.** The row is on the accountability trail: a revocation, a moderation act or a moderator's track record, a slashing verdict, a hard case.

**Why.** The trust root moderates and does not edit history. An adverse record about a root would otherwise be removable only by that root, which is its subject.

**Text.** CC 2.4.1.1: "trust-root moderation is not trust-root history-editing"

## A moderator's track record is not the moderator's to remove

**When.** The row is a participant's moderation record in a community, signed by another party.

**Why.** The record is a verdict about the party it names. If that party could withdraw the rows that count against it, the record would hold only what its subject let stand. Its signer may withdraw it; the subject contests it by reconsideration.

**Text.** CC 2.4.1.1: "the anti-Goodhart dual admits no subject-side removal of an adverse row"

## A revocation is never taken back

**When.** The row revokes a key or a device's standing under an identity.

**Why.** Revocation does not roll back. Its bound on what the revoked key signed may be lowered and never raised, so no later row restores the key.

**Text.** CC 2.4.1.1: "revocation is non-rollbackable"

## The row is an act of standing, judged at the moment it was signed

**When.** The row is an owner's, a roster's or a consenting party's act, whose authority is the signer's standing when it signed.

**Why.** The act stays good after its signer leaves, and a key that has lost standing cannot sign an act back into the time when it held it: past the freshness window such a row is refused.

**Text.** CC 3.3.6.3: "so an evicted key can backdate an owner act into its old live window by at most W, and no further"

## A revocation made before the roster door is kept where it was made

**When.** The row carries a framed revocation of an occurrence that was signed outside the roster's two paths.

**Why.** The old revocation still retires the occurrence's route on the node that holds it, and removes no device from a person's roster. For a person's own occurrence it becomes a row only when a majority of the roster cosigns, so one key cannot spread it. The table therefore holds entries with no row behind them and cannot be rebuilt from rows.

**Text.** CC 3.1.3: "is kept as a local projection that still revokes but has no attestation row"

## An accord's row names the accord it belongs to

**When.** The row is an accord's act: its standing, a halt, a notice or a drill.

**Why.** A halt or a roster act counts only toward the accord it names. A row that names no accord, or one the reader does not hold, is refused when the rows are read.

**Text.** CC 2.6.1.7: "Which accord an accord:* row belongs to; a foreign row carrying junk fails closed at the fold"

## A testimony stays with its witness

**When.** The row is a person's own account of what they saw.

**Why.** A testimony is kept at the witness's own scope, is never added up and never grounds a penalty alone, so it travels no further than the witness's own nodes.

**Text.** CC 3.1.9.3: "the four load-bearing wire-level disciplines (`witness_relation: self`, `cohort_scope: self`, never aggregated, never sole evidence for `slashing:*`)"

## A need is an open call

**When.** The row asks the whole mesh for help of a named kind.

**Why.** A call that only the caller's own circle could read would reach nobody able to answer it, so the row is signed at federation scope.

**Text.** CC 3.1.9.3: "Federation-scope open-call surface"

## A watchlist belongs to one group

**When.** The row turns on a matching list for the content of one group.

**Why.** A list that applied everywhere would let one authority screen every room. Each group's own moderating authority enables its list, and the row goes to that group and no further.

**Text.** CC 3.1.9.4: "**Per-group, NEVER global**"

## A registry-consensus attestation is world-readable

**When.** The row reports that independent registry sources agree about a key.

**Why.** A reader anywhere must be able to check a key against it before trusting the key, so the row is signed at federation scope in plain text.

**Text.** CC 4.4.3.2.1: "are therefore `cohort_scope: federation` (Commons/plaintext, world-readable)"
