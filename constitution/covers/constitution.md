# The Constitution

**What this is.** The power volume: who confers standing, who may halt, who may amend, how a person leaves, and what every named power is paired with. It gathers the admission gate (1.2), the community and trust-root rules (3.2), the accord layer and the halt (4.2), the Wise Authorities (4.3), amendment (4.5.1), the anti-patterns (4.1) and the governance discipline of moderation, redress, child safety, takedown and purge (4.5).

**Whom it binds.** The holders of the accord root, the stewards of agents, the founders and moderators of communities, the Wise who answer deferrals, and every node that admits their rows. Each power here is exercised as a signed row under a named key and is checked by another row.

**What it promises.** Every act of power is signed by a named person and can be contested. A halt is one holder's row, lapses in a day unless a majority confirms it, moves no trust state and touches nothing but an agent's act gate. A root can only tell a node to share less. Anyone may found a root and anyone may leave one. Nobody who cannot answer for themselves is on the network alone. No hand bans a community; what is not hidden is stated, and withdrawal is node by node.

**What it leaves out.** The byte shapes of the rows these powers ride on, which are in *The Wire*; the family-by-family admission rules, which are in *The Dictionary*; and the question of which of these rules have code behind them today, which only *The Evidence Register* answers.

**Where the rest lives.** The reasons behind the design are in *The Charter*. The trust assumptions a reviewer should attack are in *The Threat Model*. The formal models that check the halt, the roster and the standing verdict are named in the register's witness column.
