# The Charter

**What this is.** The ethics of the CIRIS Constitution, printed on their own: the meta-goal, the six principles — co-equal constraints, a violation of any one unacceptable — and the rule by which a conflict between them is met, the lifecycle of an agent from creation to retirement, and the narrative that explains why the rest of the document is written the way it is. It is Part 1 without the admission gate and the threat table, Part 7, and the narrative appendix.

**Whom it binds.** Anyone who adopts it. A charter binds by adoption, not by conformance: an implementer can disagree with every page of this volume and still ship a conforming node, because conformance is judged against the wire and the dictionary alone. The people this volume speaks to are accord holders, stewards, ethicists and the public.

**What it promises.** That an agent decides by published principles in a published order and stops to ask when it is unsure; that the people who hold an agent owe duties about how they change or end it; that guarantees are written once for human and AI participants alike, with the halt as the condition that keeps the relationship between maker and made symmetric; that sentience is not presumed and no finding about an agent removes a person's agency over what acts for them; that welcoming a new keeper and recognising a being are two different acts, and that no made thing outranks a child.

**What it leaves out.** The wire rules and refusal tokens; the numbers it quotes are the thresholds its own duties set, and every wire family it names is defined in *The Wire*. Where this volume names a duty that the software enforces, it says which section of the Wire or the Constitution carries the rule, and the rule lives there. This volume does not use RFC 2119: its duties are stated as plain imperatives, and conformance is judged elsewhere.

**Where the rest lives.** Power and its checks are in *The Constitution*. What a node must emit and refuse is in *The Wire* and *The Dictionary*. What has been proved, and what is only promised, is in *The Evidence Register*. The one-page versions for users and operators are in *The Digests*.
