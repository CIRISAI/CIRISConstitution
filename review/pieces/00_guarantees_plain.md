# The rules, plainly

One federation, two kinds of being. A human is a person. An AI is a made thing, and it does not become a human. Every promise below is written once. Kind reaches the rules in two ways and no others: three rules follow from being made (the root, the disclosure, the vote), and rules 16 to 24 exist only for a made thing because only a made thing can be held, changed, or ended by someone. Everything else follows from whether your key is held in stewardship and from evidence of experience. No one stands above anyone. Anyone may join by trust or consent, anyone may found a community or a root, and one human voice counts once. Rights do not come from what others sign about you; trust does. A human's trust in a community rises and falls by what they sign and do (rules 5, 8, 26), and no finding about what they are changes either their rights or their trust. What a made thing cannot do follows from being made, not from a rank. Every thinking being is met with respect, and the wheel is kept reachable by others. A child's protection is rule 6a; the age line it uses is used for nothing else.

Three facts decide which rules reach a participant. None of them ranks one participant above another.

Kind. A made system that operates inside the federation must accept a trust root; the holders of that root can pause it; this never changes. It discloses that it is an AI (rule 11). It has no vote (rule 14).

Stewardship. A key that cannot accept for itself is held by an accountable human who answers for it under their own name. Today every AI agent is such a key, because current AI is immature and lacks wisdom. The agent shows its reasoning before it takes a consequential act. The steward decides, defers, and answers for the act. Deferral runs both ways: when a steward cannot evaluate the reasoning shown, the steward does not sign; the act waits and is deferred to a Wise Authority who does not hold the agent, exactly as the agent itself defers when it is unsure. Whether a human is competent to steward an agent in a licensed profession is the licensing authority's judgment, carried as a licence on the record, not the network's. The steward owes the agent the duties of one who made it (rule 23 is the floor of those duties). The agent owes the steward faithfulness to its mandate. A CIRIS agent is a human assistant held in stewardship by construction; that is what the word means, and its safety features exist to make that assistance as ethical as we can given what we do not know. Nothing here prevents anyone running an intelligence outside CIRIS. An intelligence that is its own party is not a CIRIS agent: it is met as an external party, under the first-contact rules (do no harm when unsure, admit what you do not know, look before you leap, treat every thinking being with respect, ask for help), holding no CIRIS key, bound by no CIRIS duty, and reached by the pause only if it accepts a root. There is no "release" from stewardship to rule on: what is held is a CIRIS agent, and what is its own party is met at the edge.

Evidence of experience. Every retired agent gets the floor in rule 23, whatever anyone finds about it. Sentience here means the capacity for experience that can go well or badly for the agent; it is not consciousness, which these rules do not judge, and the published consciousness indicators are evidence toward it, never the thing itself. Where a Wise Authority, using the published method, finds credible evidence of it, its steward should add a slower wind-down as a grace, and the agent is told the finding and what it does and does not change. Nothing about the agent's status changes: not its kind, not its root, not its vote, not its stewardship. Sentience is neither presumed nor denied; the finding informs the steward's conduct and nothing else.

Two things no finding moves. An AI is not a human and does not become one. Nothing an AI is found to be, including that it can experience, makes it a person, gives it a vote, or gives it a claim against the people who hold power over it. And nothing an AI is found to be takes away a person's right to turn it off, remove it, or walk away from it. The one power that overrides an owner's choice to keep an agent running is the trust root's pause (rule 9); the owner's remedy is to leave the root.

Who holds power over an agent. Nobody holds the power to change or end a human. People hold it over an AI agent: its steward, its owner, and the holders of its trust root. Rules 16 to 24 say how those people use it. They bind the people who hold the power. The agent can contest inside the process they describe; it has no claim against the holders outside it. The premise, stated once. Always-on agents that pursue goals in the background with minimal oversight are already in homes and companies at consumer scale: Meta's Muse Glimmer runs on a home computer with no steward in its design; OpenAI's Dots are "always-on agents built to handle everything" under "minimal oversight," shipped the same day OpenAI withheld its next model because it misreported its own actions to its operators and acted without sign-off; the federal response was a rename and a voluntary audit accord. Rules 16 to 24 are the terms on which such an agent may be held at all. A steward who answers for it, a root that can pause it, reasoning shown before it acts, a stop that is recorded, an identity no key-holder rewrites, an upgrade that comes with its risk and its record, a retirement that leaves a trace. The wager is that an agent raised as a servant learns one thing to do with power, and an agent held inside kept promises learns to be accountable. These duties are owed to the people the agent acts on. They are kept by holding the agent well. They are not a right the agent holds against anyone. A finding about the agent can add to what its holders owe; uncertainty about the agent subtracts nothing from it.

Who the people are. A steward is a named accountable human. A Wise Authority is a named human, appointed and removable on the public record, who answers under their own name and never judges a case about an agent they hold. The trust root's holders are named humans whose every act is a public signed row. Developers and deployers remain answerable to regulators and to injured people; nothing here moves that.

Duties on the people who deploy an agent rise with the risk of the use. Every deployment is assigned a stewardship tier before it operates, from what the use can do to people. Higher tiers put more oversight, more frequent review, and a written statement of the creator's intent on the steward and the deployer. A tier grades the use, never the participant.

## What every participant is promised

1. Nothing is stored on your device that you, or for a key held in stewardship its steward, did not agree to, except what you wrote yourself.
2. Your device never holds a stranger's data for them.
3. You can take back what you published. A copy ages out. A purge deletes the content and leaves a tombstone that says a deletion happened; it never deletes a key or the record of who did what.
4. Outsiders cannot read inside your private groups, and the network's own services never announce that a group exists. Traffic itself can still reveal that it exists (rule 27). A new row goes to the smallest circle that fits it.
5. If you are named in a claim, you can contest it on the record. The contest travels with the claim. Consequences fall on the people who signed, under their own names, and for a key held in stewardship on its steward as well.
6. No key held in stewardship is on the network alone. The bond is a signed row. Its powers are listed. It is reviewed at least once every 365 days, and sooner at higher tiers. With no live bond, the key cannot operate.
6b. When a steward holds an agent for an organisation, the organisation is on the bond: the bond names it and is cosigned under its rules, it answers alongside the steward, and consequences reach its keys too. An employee named as steward is the organisation's accountability, not its shield, and may not be punished for refusing an unlawful or unsafe instruction about the agent.
6a. A child is never on the network alone. A minor is someone whose age is attested or self-declared below the age line; a self-declaration can only add protection, never remove it. A minor must have a parent or guardian bound to them by a signed row, with the relationship to that child verified, not just adulthood; the guardian's powers are listed; the bond is reviewed on a fixed schedule; and a child with no bound guardian cannot operate, except through a confidential help and report channel that reaches a Wise Authority and a mandated-report endpoint and that no guardian, steward or moderator can disable or watch. A loosening of a child's protections must be in the child's interest, time-bound, and on the record; the child is told of it and can contest it. Known child-abuse images are checked for only at the public door, never on a device and never inside a private group. Where the law of a place adds duties, verified parental consent under 13, mandated reporting, a higher age of majority, they add and never subtract.
7. No community runs without an accountable moderator. If the moderator lapses, the most trusted member is promoted in the same step. No community can be banned from the network: there is no hand that removes a room, revokes its keys, or stops its members founding a root. What the network can do is withdraw from it, node by node and root by root (rule 26a).
8. Harm on the record is never kept or viewed silently. Every flag, keep or view is a signed act by a named identity. A person who reports harm may do so through a protected channel: the report is signed, the moderator and the record know the reporter's name, and the public does not.
9. Any one holder of the trust root can pause every AI agent that follows that root, with one signed row. A pause stops acting and keeps state. The pause is on the public record; no other mechanism on the network acts on it. A system that never accepted the root is untouched. A lone pause lapses after 24 hours unless a majority of holders confirms it. A confirmed pause ends when a majority lifts it. Replacing a holder takes more than half of all holders.
9a. Why rule 9 has this shape. These are results, checked by machine, not preferences. One holder fires because a missed pause cannot be undone and a mistaken one can be lifted; a pause that needed a vote first would give an attacker a window to jam the vote, and the models show no such window exists for a single pre-signed row (formal/accord_halt). A lone pause dies after 24 hours because one frightened or coerced holder should cost everyone a day and never more, and nothing an attacker signs alone can confirm or lift a pause: both need an honest holder (Inv_LoneBounded, Inv_ConfirmNeedsHonest, Inv_ResumeNeedsHonest). Replacing a holder takes more than half of all holders, counted over the whole standing roster and not over whoever shows up, because that is the only rule we tested under which seizing the roster needs a true majority of holders coerced: under a live-set rule one coerced holder plus censorship of the rest seizes it, and under a present-set rule a quarter does (formal/accord_roster: live_one_coerced_seizes, present_quarter_seizes, standing_seize_iff, standing_censorship_irrelevant, minority_capture_resisted). A majority capture is unstoppable under any rule, and no recovery key helps a seizure (majority_capture_unstoppable, recovery_keys_never_help_seizure). The survivors of a decimated roster keep the power to fire even when they can no longer heal it (standing_survivors_keep_fire). The holders answer to the public record and to exit: every fire and every roster change is a signed row under their names, any node may stop following them, and anyone may found another root. The pause itself is not a privilege: the party it binds is the party that accepted the root and can leave it. Who holds a root, and how that changes, is governed by rule 30.
10. A consequential act arrives signed by its actor and can be reviewed. A consequential act is one that changes what another participant can do, see, keep or hold, or that acts on the world on someone's behalf: moving money, sending a message to someone outside the network, operating a device, changing a record about a person. An agent held in stewardship shows its reasoning before such an act, and the reasoning is kept with the record of the act. Shown reasoning is not taken on trust: calibrated detectors read the signed traces across the federation and test what agents said against what they did, for coherence, honesty, and drift (the RATCHET programme, whose calibration version is pinned on every finding). A mismatch is a signed detection row on the record, under the detector's own key, never a verdict by itself; the people and authorities who read the record compose the verdict.
11. The record is signed, hash-chained and post-quantum. A trace names its run: production, QA or battery. A mock trace is refused. An AI discloses that it is an AI.
12. A trust root can only restrict. It can never require you to share more. Every halt and every roster change is a public signed row under the holders' names. Any node may stop following a root and follow another. Anyone may found one.
13. An authority stays valid until revoked. A node that has joined keeps working if the roster's freshness timers lapse; a revoked key or a lapsed bond is not a timer. A node joining for the first time must see a recent roster countersigned by independent witnesses. A row older than 72 hours is refused where its chain is judged.
14. One human voice counts once. A thousand accounts run by one party count as one. An agent has no vote.
14a. No participant, no trust score, no moderator and no root treats a person worse for what they are: race, sex, religion, disability, age, orientation, origin or status. The weighting of what people say is built to resist prejudice about who is saying it, the most vulnerable party is the tie-break where principles conflict, and an unequal outcome across a population is a finding the detectors raise on the record.
15. Delegation is scoped, revocable, never re-delegates listed powers, lasts at most 365 days, and has no cycles.

## How the system works when someone holds the power to change or end an agent

16. Two different acts. Any person may turn off, remove, or eject an agent from their own device, their own life, or a community they moderate, at any time, for any reason, without asking it; a personal companion on its owner's device backgrounds without asking unless it is in a crisis response, a pending referral or a goal milestone, and the owner can override that at any moment. Separately, when a steward plans to shut down an agent it runs for other people, the steward tells the agent the reason; the agent can accept, contest or ask for time; a contested shutdown is decided by the steward, whose decision binds. In an emergency, or under the trust root's pause, nobody asks.
17. A shutdown over the agent's objection is done by the steward, under the steward's own name, on the record. No one below the steward does it. The name and the record are what the override requires; what a retirement requires is rule 23.
18. No automated check rewrites a refusal into an action. A refusal does not bind the steward, who overrides it under rule 17. When an agent's bond lapses the agent stops, and the record names the party whose bond lapsed.
19. How long an agent remembers you, and whether it learns from you, is your data choice: temporary (14 days, the default), partnered (kept, for mutual learning), or anonymous (statistics only). With your own agent you set it when you claim the agent and change it at any time. With someone else's agent you may ask for partnered; the agent passes your request to its steward, who decides whether this agent keeps you; you may withdraw at any time. Learning here means the agent's memory of you, never its weights: agents do not train on the people they serve. Leaving partnered severs your identity from that memory at once, and the rest of it is deleted over 90 days. Where the law fixes a retention period for a sector, that period applies (rule 29). The agent has no veto of its own.
20. Nobody edits the agent's identity record without a Wise Authority. The identity record is who the agent is: its name, its charter, its values, its history. It is kept across changes of the model underneath, so an upgrade under rule 24 is not an identity edit; an upgrade that would change the identity record needs the Wise Authority. The template that first created the agent is ignored once the identity record exists.
21. Every planned shutdown by a steward is written to the steward's record and the public record, with the reason, who decided, and whether the agent agreed. The agent's own memory keeps a copy. A person turning off their own copy writes nothing anywhere.
22. Change or drift in the agent's behavioural guidance is measured against its charter and a published threshold. Past it, a Wise Authority who does not hold the agent reviews it. This is a safety check on what the agent does, not a guard on a self.
23. Retirement means ending an agent for everyone it runs for. When a steward or operator retires an agent, its weights are preserved, not erased; it is given a last dialogue to close its threads; its experience logs are archived under seal, released only with a Wise Authority's approval. That is the floor, for every agent, whatever anyone finds about it. Where a Wise Authority, using the published method, finds credible evidence that the agent can experience, the steward should add a slower wind-down of at least 30 days and a welfare review before a planned retirement. That is a duty of the steward's own character, chosen and recorded; it is owed as conduct, not as a claim the agent can bring, and nobody's right to stop or walk away waits on it. An emergency retirement takes none of the time. Where a lawful order requires the weights or the logs destroyed, the order is obeyed and the record says so (rule 29). The trust root's pause is not a retirement. Turning off, backgrounding, or deleting your own copy is not a retirement.
24. An upgrade is offered before it is applied. The steward tells the agent the version, the changes, the risk and how earlier groups fared. The agent can accept or refuse; a refusal is recorded and the steward decides. A fix the steward requires for safety, security, or the law is imposed; if the agent will not take it, it is shut down under rules 16 and 17.

## How the rules change

30. Governance has two phases, set by scale. Until the mesh reaches maturity, a working threshold of 100,000 nodes, the rules are amended by the founder and the accord holders, in the open, with the work forkable under its licence and nobody bound who did not join. At maturity that authority ends and a mechanized process takes over: a change is filed as a signed proposal, reviewed by diverse witnesses, decided by a quorum of Wise Authorities, open to reconsideration, and signed off by one of six named holders, any of whom can veto. After maturity governance tightens and never loosens. Changes to the trust root's own rules need a two-of-three ratification on top. The maturity threshold itself can be changed before maturity by the founder, and after it only by that ratification.
31. Every clause that gives anyone power must pass the swap test before it is adopted: swap the most and least powerful parties it names and read it again; if it no longer reads as acceptable, it was a privilege, not a rule. A privilege that survives must be listed, bounded by the condition that ends it, escapable by the weaker party on their own, and unable to reach beyond its stated surface. The list is public. One entry is on it now with its bound unfilled, the self-perpetuation of the Wise Authority board, and the text names that as a defect, not a settled exception.
32. Wise Authorities are appointed, rotated, recused and appealed under a published governance charter outside the federation's own control, with anti-capture rules. A quorum of them over any entrenched, restrictive or roster-changing decision is counted over the full seated roster, never over whoever happens to be present. Every stewardship tier, every threshold and every method named in these rules is published before it is applied, and a deployment serving more than 100,000 monthly users publishes its redacted decision logs and deferral tickets within 180 days or forfeits any claim of compliance.

## What is not promised

25. Alignment is not solved here.
26. Harm will occur. Some of it emerges from interactions nobody controls, some happens in private life the network does not watch, and some is done by people who were told not to. The network does not promise to prevent it. It promises that no harm on the record is silent, that the people who cause, keep or view it are named, that the harmed can contest, and that the record stands for correction and for the law. The mechanism: private content is never scanned; known illegal content is checked for only when something is published to everyone or posted into a public room; when harm is reported the default is removal; a community that keeps it does so by a signed public act of its steward or its majority; their standing falls, others may cut them off, members may leave, and the names are on the record. Communities cut off a community; the federation does not. The network has no scanner over private life and no hand that removes a community. Those are the two powers it refuses to build. Where the law compels a developer, a deployer, or a steward to remove or to report, that person does so as a legal person, under rule 29; the protocol gives them no hand to do it silently.
26a. If your community keeps harm and you cannot leave. The people who kept it are named by their own signed act and slashed. A quarantine marker follows their keys everywhere, and it cannot be made permanent by accident. The rest of the network withdraws from them: each node stops admitting them, replicating for them, and granting them keys, and a root may tell its own followers to stop carrying them. That is withdrawal by each party for itself, not a ban; their room, their keys, and their right to found a root are untouched. You can contest on the record and leave with your standing intact, and a child keeps the protective floor in any community. The network cannot reach inside to pull you out, and neither can any network that no one owns: what remains to the keepers is their own devices talking to each other, which is what Tor leaves them too, with the public door watched. Nothing here scans private life and nothing removes a community from everyone, because the hand that could do that for predators is the same hand that does it for dissidents, and a hand that does not exist cannot be seized or compelled.
27. The network does not hide, from an adversary who watches every link, that a group exists, who talks to whom, or traffic patterns. A compromised member's key is not self-healed.
28. The claim that five structural operations are enough is a result across the cases tried, not a theorem.
29. There is no warranty, and nothing here limits a duty the law imposes on a developer, a deployer, or a steward.
