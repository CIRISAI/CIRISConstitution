## Major new findings

- **`audience`** (5.2,6.1.5.3; condition). CC 5.2 "all current identity_occurrences of C.attesting_key_id" against CC 6.1.5.3 "is not counted toward the target, is not a deficit, receives no keys for the cohort and pulls nothing for it". CC 5.2's enforcement block has the substrate MUST wrap the content key to every current occurrence at self and every member at family; CC 6.1.5.3 gives a node its owner denied (and a server-class node by default) no key. The 5.2 prose paragraph 'Which of a member's nodes' sides with 6.1.5.3; the pseudo-code and the 'Composition with the at-rest encryption flow' paragraph do not carry the allow list.
- **`audience`** (5.2,4.4.3.2.1,5.4.2; scope). CC 5.2 "the listing, the holding claims and, by asking a member's node, the content; never the roster" against CC 4.4.3.2.1 "opt OUT of the mandatory DEK cascade and are Commons-tier (plaintext, holding claims at federation scope, no DEK)". CC 5.2's table gives a public room's rows the audience 'the claimed nodes of the roster's members', and a node holds a row only when in its audience; CC 4.4.3.2.1 and 5.4.2 call the room Commons-tier, whose audience in the same table is every node. Whether a non-member node may hold and re-serve a public room's content differs. Beside C254 (discoverability), which is not this.
- **`audience`** (6.1.5,6.1.5.3,5.4.6; term). CC 6.1.5 "(governed by LRU-only): no holding claim, no compress request, no rarest-first biasing" against CC 5.4.6 "hides who published a federation-scope item from the nodes that store it, from the directory, and, with shaping, from an observer of the network". 'Anonymous tier' names two things. In CC 6.1.5 (SR-2), 6.1.5.3, 6.1 (WW-2) and 6.1.8 (RB-1) it is a class of content with no holding claim, outside the witness and exempt from anti-entropy; in CC 5.4.6 it is an onion transport for federation-scope items that are stored and listed in the directory. Whether an item published through the tier carries holding claims and is replicated differs by which section is read.
- **`audience`** (5.4.6; condition). CC 5.4.6 "A node offers an `identity:occurrence:v1` row to a peer only when it would also relay the row's attester key to that peer" against CC 5.4.6 "key records and identity occurrences, which are public and are what let the two peers seal to each other". The dependency closure withholds an occurrence whose attester's owner-binding is not announced at federation scope; the forwarder's 'serves openly' list and the stranger-carried set ('key records and revocations, occurrences') name occurrences with no such condition. An unannounced node's occurrence is served to a stranger under one reading and withheld under the other. The 'never listed' MUST of the same section supports the closure.

- **`supersede`** (2.4.1,3.2,3.5.1; actor). CC 2.4.1 "This attestation row replaces a prior one by the same attester" against CC 3.2 "a new guardian's `delegates_to` carried as a `supersedes` ([CC 2.4.1.1](part_2_the_grammar.md)) replaces the prior binding". - **`supersede`** (2.4.1,3.5.1,3.4.5.1,4.4.3.3.1,5.2; term). CC 3.4.5.1 "it does not un-say the prior record, it ends its liveness" against CC 3.5.1 "carries the prior's `asserted_at` and is a placement, never a competing successor". A supersedes ends the prior row's liveness (CC 2.4.1 'replaces', CC 3.4.5.1); a widening is a supersedes (CC 4.4.3.3.1) yet is a placement that leaves the narrower row standing (CC 3.5.1, CC 5.2 boundary case, CC 3.2). A reader of CC 4.4.3.3.1 alone retires the narrower row on widening; whether the owner's self copy stays live and whether un-widening is possible differ. C132 and C133 are two instances of this at the owner-binding.
- **`licensure`** (7.3.3,8.8.3; condition). CC 7.3.3 "any system assessed at **RM ≥ 4** opens the Catastrophic-Risk Evaluation. That is the one trigger" against CC 8.8.3 "A system must pass a CRE before deployment if it meets any of:". CC 7.3.3 (three times) makes RM ≥ 4 the one trigger of the Catastrophic-Risk Evaluation and Annex D only the criteria it applies; Annex D-1 is headed 'Trigger criteria' and requires the evaluation on compute above 10^26 FLOP, transactional authority above $10M a day, or a recursive event, with no mention of RM. A low-RM system that meets D-1, or an RM 4 system that meets none of it, is evaluated under one and not the other.
- **`licensure`** (2.4.1.2.1,4.4.3.8,4.4.3.2.8; condition). CC 2.4.1.2.1 "`authority_id` names a key: a `federation_keys.key_id`, or an organisation" against CC 4.4.3.8 "Its own walk from its own edges reaches the issuing authority for the row's class, and that authority's charter declares the class.". CC 2.4.1.2.1 and CC 4.4.3.4.3 let anyone be a licence authority under a bare key; CC 4.4.3.8 honours a licence only where the authority's charter declares the class, and CC 4.4.3.2.8 carries that declaration on a signed community record. A bare key has no charter, so its licences are honoured by no reader under 4.4.3.8 and folded as licensure under 2.4.1.2.1. The CC 4.3 seat rule ('holds a live licence from that authority') does not say which reading it takes.
- **`roster_fold`** (3.2,3.3.6.3,4.5.12.2; condition). CC 3.2 "a strict majority of the live roster, the proposer included, or the lone path after its window with no objection" against CC 3.3.6.3 "elapses and no strict majority of the *other* members objects". CC 3.2 and CC 4.5.12.2 let the lone path land only with no objection; CC 3.3.6.3 lets it land unless a strict majority of the other keys object. With three keys and one objecting, a new key is admitted (or a key evicted) under 3.3.6.3 and not under 3.2. The policy rows self_roster_add and self_roster_evict (cancel: 'more than 1/2 of the others') support CC 3.3.6.3.
- **`consent_fold`** (6.1.5,6.1.5.2,6.1.5.3; actor). CC 6.1.5 "Durability is a **per-item choice the owner makes on publish**." against CC 6.1.5.2 "Consent *authorizes*, the owner *elects* — a pin requires **both**.". 'Owner' and 'durable' name two things. In CC 6.1.5 and 6.1.5.1 the item's publisher marks it durable and the swarm replicates it to the target across the cohort's live nodes; in CC 6.1.5.2 content is durable on a node only if that node's owner lists its class in `pinned_class`, and is otherwise cache that descends first. CC 6.1.5.3 runs both at every tier without saying which governs when a member's node has not elected the class: whether that node counts as a holder and keeps the item differs.
