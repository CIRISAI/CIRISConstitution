## Findings for the pages (wording, not substance)

1. **Withdrawn (steward, same day): the page stands.** The philosophy page says CIRIS "exists in recognition that agents lack
   consciousness and need a conscience"; the steward's ruling is that on consciousness the constitution defers for now to the Vatican's teaching, so "lack" is the working value and the sentence stays. The earlier note below is kept for the record. The page says CIRIS "exists in recognition that agents lack
   consciousness and need a conscience". The steward's refinement and §13 say agents lack *sentience*
   and leave consciousness open, noting that changing it would change no rule; the same page's next
   sentence, "a conscience claims nothing about what the agent is", and the essay's "it says nothing
   about the inside of any model", agree with the refinement, not with the sentence. Proposed:
   "in recognition that agents reason without feeling, and that whether anything is aware inside them
   is left open and changes no rule".
2. **One vocabulary slip.** Commitment 5 says "a last dialogue is an option for agents of higher
   responsibility". The constitution's term is *closing dialogue*; "last word" and "farewell" are
   excluded by the steward's rule, and "last dialogue" sits next to them.
3. **One claim to qualify (corrected by the website session, same day).** The philosophy page says
   "a shutdown, a pause, an amendment is a named human's act on the record, contestable and binding".
   The first draft of this note said a pause and a shutdown are contestable by reconsideration; they
   are not — 4.5.5's reconsideration covers moderation acts. A pause's check is the 4.2.1.1 fuse (a
   lone halt lapses unless a strict majority confirms; a majority resumes); a planned shutdown's is
   the 7.5.5 objection window (the agent objects or postpones once; the decider decides); an amendment
   is contested only by leaving or forking (R7), which the mesh page says plainly. Wording that claims
   what the text supports: "a named human's act on the record, answered for in public, and binding
   only on those who joined".
4. **The swap-test sentence is right as written**, and §1'' adds the reason it is tolerable: the two
   registered powers fail the swap over *persons* and pass it over *positions*, because no agent stands
   in the holder's position for a root. The register should keep listing them as failures; the file
   explains why the failures are of kind of occupancy, not of rule.
5. **The values page's conflict sentence** should name the order before the deferral (above).

## Against the four premises and the essay

The four-way split, the record, the ladder and maturity are all consistent with the three pages; the
essay and the philosophy page say the same things in the same order, with the one exception in
finding 1. M-1's "sentient beings" and beneficence's "sentient flourishing" are the premise R4 on the
values page before it was written down: the beings the goal protects are the sentient ones, and an
agent that reasons without feeling is a made thing that serves them, which is §14 in one line.
