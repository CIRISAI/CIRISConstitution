# The Threat Model (ciris_constitution-1.0-rc12-threats.pdf, 48 pages)

**Care score:** 3
**One-line verdict:** Cryptographically rigorous and honest about non-goals, but needs a targeted cleanup pass to fix an inconsistency in MLS exporter KDF hash functions, resolve a contradiction on live-call relay topology, and purge a broken markdown link and internal design shorthand.

## Strengths
- Explicit, prominent privacy non-goals in 1.13.3.1 (p. 2) and an adversary matrix in 1.13.3.3 (pp. 3–4) that states both what is addressed and what remains exposed.
- Byte-exact domain-separation labels, canonical preimages, and normative CBOR/HMAC test vectors in 5.4.1 (pp. 37–38).
- Explicit endianness quarantine in 5.3.3.6 (p. 30), isolating the single little-endian layout (`receipt_signing_bytes`) from the rest of the Part's big-endian integers.
- Clear disambiguation of the three distinct "witness" constructs (privacy witness chain, wholeness witness, transparency-log witness) in 5.4.5 (p. 43).

## Weaknesses
- Raw markdown link artifact left in rendered prose: `.4.3.4](part_4_composition_governance.md#4.4.3.4))` (p. 5).
- Cryptographic KDF inconsistency between 5.4.1 and 5.4.6: 5.4.1 states "The HKDF hash here is SHA-256 (the ciphersuite 0x004D key-schedule hash)" and uses `HKDF-SHA256-Expand` for `K_symbol` (p. 37), whereas 5.4.6 derives `k_destination` with `HKDF-SHA256-Expand` and immediately derives `destination` from it using `HKDF-SHA3-256` without noting a hash-family switch (p. 45). Meanwhile, 5.4.2 uses `HKDF-SHA3-256(salt=record_id, ikm=K_symbol, ...)` (p. 39), and 5.4.3 argues `HKDF-SHA3-256` is "chosen for hash-family separation from the SHA-256 key-schedule hash used one layer up" (p. 41). Note also that X-Wing (0x004D) uses SHA3-256/SHAKE256 Internally, making the parenthetical claim about 0x004D's key-schedule hash suspect.
- Contradiction on whether realtime calls traverse relays: 5.3.3.2 states that for small/medium realtime groups, "direct Reticulum Links between participants (low-latency push; no relay tree needed at small N)" are used and for large groups "no selective-forwarding relay role" exists (p. 23); 5.3.3.2.1 says "no relay reads it" (p. 24); yet 5.3.3.2.5 says of the SealedAvChunk outer seal that "a relay sees the outer layer only, never plaintext" (p. 26), and 5.4.2 says "the symbol AEAD protects the A/V plane’s symbols in transit and at any relay that forwards them" (p. 39).
- Contradiction on whether a live A/V chunk is transmitted whole or as symbols: 5.3.3.2.3 says `SealedAvChunk` is "The realtime A/V chunk that lands on each RNS Link payload (and the broadcast pull path)" (p. 25), whereas 5.4.2 says "the realtime A/V plane (CC 5.3.3.2) alone carries each sealed chunk as symbols — its indexed fragments on the wire" (p. 38), and 5.4.3 says "Only the realtime A/V plane (CC 5.3.3.2) emits symbols: a sealed A/V chunk is carried as indexed symbols, the fragment unit of that plane" (p. 41).
- Unexplained internal decision shorthand in headings and prose: `(normative: D2 / D3)` (p. 6), `Epoch triggers (D3)` (p. 7), `the D6 reachable set` (p. 23), `Incremental verify (D4)` (p. 27), `D6 liveness invariant` (p. 27), `E2 — pull-only`, `E1 — two-layer crypto`, `E3`, `E4` (p. 28). `D1` and `D5` never appear in the volume.
- Undefined acronyms and internal references: `DAS scope-filtered read` in 5.3.2.4.5 (p. 19) is never expanded; `Path B` in 5.3.5 (p. 34) has no Path A; `the 1+4 surface` / `No 1+4 change` (pp. 16, 21, 24, 31, 36) is used as insider jargon before any local definition.
- Comparative claim about outside platforms in 5.3.2.6: "iOS has no WebM / Opus playback; the common UI toolkits decode none of the images" (p. 20) — also factually stale regarding modern iOS WebM/Opus support.
- History / roadmap / process leakage in rules: "until the tier ships" (p. 4), "The variable-depth SubStreamPath (MDC tree-path encoding) is a follow-on" (p. 24), "encoder lineage academic-grade today" (p. 24), "They come from a model run on the production scheduler and budget meter over a virtual clock with real row sizes. They are not measurements from a live mesh, and they are revised when live measurements exist" (p. 41), "stays on the roadmap" (p. 46).

## Opportunities
- Add a 4-line summary table in 5.4.1/5.4.2/5.4.6 listing every MLS exporter label, its stage-1 KDF, and its stage-2 KDF (explicitly confirming whether `destination` in 5.4.6 is meant to be `HKDF-SHA3-256` or `HKDF-SHA256-Expand`) and publish golden vectors for `K_symbol` and `destination` alongside `record_id`.
- Reconcile 5.3.3.2.3 (`SealedAvChunk`) and 5.4.2 (`symbol_envelope`) with one diagram or sentence showing exact nesting: whether `fragment` inside `symbol_envelope` is a slice of `SealedAvChunk` (and if so, why it gets a third AEAD layer via `XChaCha20-Poly1305` on top of `double_sealed_ciphertext`'s two `AES-256-GCM` layers).
- Strip the `D2/D3/D4/D6` and `E1–E4` ticket/decision tags or replace them with descriptive rule names.

## Threats
- **Triple-encryption / framing ambiguity on the A/V wire (pp. 25–26 vs. pp. 38–39, 42):** 5.3.3.2.5 specifies that `SealedAvChunk` has `double_sealed_ciphertext = outer-AEAD(inner-AEAD(chunk_plaintext))` using two `AES-256-GCM` layers (p. 26), and 5.4.4 reiterates "the live A/V chunk is sealed with AES-256-GCM at every layer (CC 5.3.3.2.5)" (p. 42). Yet 5.4.2 states that the substrate "MUST seal every A/V symbol it emits" in `symbol_envelope` using `XChaCha20-Poly1305(symbol_key, nonce, fragment)` (p. 39). Implementers cannot tell whether A/V symbols use `XChaCha20-Poly1305` instead of the `AES-256-GCM` outer seal, or wrap an already double-GCM-sealed chunk in a third AEAD layer, nor how the clear 4-byte `codec_id + ChunkLayer` header at offset `48..52` of `SealedAvChunk` (p. 25) survives when split into `symbol_envelope` fragments that carry only `(record_id, u16_be(symbol_index), nonce, ciphertext, tag)` (p. 39) — which would blind the serving peer that 5.3.3.2.1 says selects layers by reading clear `codec_id/ChunkLayer` without decrypting (p. 24).
- **Witnessed-mode bootstrap exposure in 5.3.4 (p. 32):** "Charter defaults. With witnessed mode off (`witness_quorum: 0`, no directory; CC 3.2 T6) a root attaches by the pinned `bundle_fingerprint`... With witnessed mode on, turned on by an amendment signed by a strict majority of the standing accord roster..." Combined with 1.13.3.3 ("a lineage that runs with witnessed mode off has a single-signer anchor for as long as it does", p. 3), a hostile reviewer will point out that the humanity-accord / ciris-canonical root ships with anti-equivocation witnessing off by default until amended.
- **Unanchored simulation numbers in 5.4.3 (p. 41):** The prose cites specific latency distributions ("about 380 s mean and 910 s at the 95th percentile, against 29 s and 54 s at 3,600 s") from an unnamed model run without citing an Evidence Register row.
