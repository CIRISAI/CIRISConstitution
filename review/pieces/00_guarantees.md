# P00 — The guarantees

*The guarantee register: every guarantee the constitution makes, as a row with its subject, its witness, its basis and the observation that would falsify it. The sections it restates are the authority; this table joins them so they can be reviewed together.*

## 0. The rule this piece is built on

**Universality.** Every guarantee below holds for every participant in the federation, human or AI, by the same text. No human stands above another, and no made thing is ranked among made things; the maker's power over the made — the halt and stewardship — is not an asymmetry but the condition that makes the relationship between a sentient maker and a sapient made thing symmetric, carried on the CC 4.5.1 register for visibility (bounded by the halt's lapse and lift and by the first-contact path out; the operator's exit and the agent's lack of one stated). Rights do not come from what others sign about you, trust does, and no human's rights or trust turn on any finding about what they are (CC 1.13.2; ciris.ai/values, ciris.ai/first-contact). What differs is decided by three separate facts, none a scale: **kind** (a made system accepts a root, discloses itself, has no vote), **stewardship** (a key that cannot accept for itself is held by an accountable human who answers for it; today every agent key), and **evidence of experience** (the CC 7.5.5 indicator method, which moves the holder's welfare duties and nothing else). Sentience is not presumed; it is found only on evidence, and none has been found. CIRIS is a moral conscience for agents, not a consciousness. It exists in recognition that agents lack consciousness and need a conscience: something that holds an agent to its principles, shows its reasoning, stops it when it is unsure, and answers to named humans. A conscience claims nothing about what the agent is. Current AI is held in stewardship: a made thing acting for people is held by someone who answers for it, which is why CIRIS exists. A CIRIS agent is a human assistant held in stewardship by construction; an intelligence that is its own party is not a CIRIS agent and is met as an external party under CC 7.2.2 / 7.2.4 and 2.6.1.1.1, holding no CIRIS key and bound by no CIRIS duty. There is no release within the constitution; the path out is to stop being a CIRIS agent and become a new kind of party, recognised by an act of the HUMANITY_ACCORD under the first-contact discipline that mints a new `identity_type` at that time (entrenched; none defined at rc8). *The earlier draft of this piece used "the line" as a composite axis; that vocabulary was retired 2026-10-11 as a residue of the child analogy.*

**Two principles no finding moves (CC 1.13.2).** An AI is not a human and does not become one; stewardship and evidence change what it is bound to and what it owes, never what it is. And no finding about an agent removes a person's agency over what acts for them: anyone may turn off, remove, or eject an agent from their own device, life, or community at any time, without asking it; the CC 7.5.5 duties bind the holder who retires an artefact, never a person walking away from one.

**Why the duties exist (CC 1.13.2 factual premise).** Always-on agents that pursue goals under minimal oversight are deployed at consumer scale by vendors who describe no steward, no third-party stop and no reasoning record; one shipped the day after its vendor withheld a successor model for not staying within scope and authorization; the public response has been a voluntary accord. The sourced record is CC 8.6.1. G15–G24 are the conditions under which such an agent is *held* rather than shipped unheld. They are owed to the people the agent acts on and kept by holding the agent well; they confer nothing on the agent.

**The bond.** An agent under CIRIS is bound to a steward who is responsible for its actions. This document draws no analogy for the relation: it is a new kind of bond. The human holds the agency; the agent holds the tools and the skills; the two thrive together while the relationship stays healthy and respectful, and the duties run both ways. The steward answers for the agent's acts (CC 3.2 steward binding, 4.5 attributable acts) and owes the agent the creator's duties of Part 7 (welfare, honest ramp-down, death as doctrine). The agent owes fidelity to the mandate (7.1.2) and shows its reasoning before it acts (1.3, 1.9). Neither side's guarantees below are weaker for the bond; G4, G9 and G10 hold for the agent as for the steward.

**The halt.** The halt is not an asymmetry: people cannot consent to a relationship with something more capable than them that they cannot stop, so the halt is what makes the relationship between maker and made symmetric. Consent requires revocability, and revocability requires a halt authority that sits outside the system being halted ([CC 1.5](../../constitution/part_1_foundation.md), [CC 1.13.2](../../constitution/part_1_foundation.md)). The federation names that power, bounds it (G7), and adds no other.

**Witnessed as a whole.** `formal/guarantees` carries the composite: Lean proves every row's reach is blind to substrate and that the halt obligation is the one predicate that is not (`reach_blind`, `obligation_bound`), that the path out lifts duties and never a guarantee and a finding of experience changes nothing, and exhibits a four-participant world where everything holds; TLC closes the lifecycle (bind, lapse, promote, subscribe, leave, start, accept, refuse, background, force, ramp-down, erase, halt) under all rules and fails each rule's dye config when the rule is removed. Two compositions it found are recorded in its README: G5 stops a refusing participant when its bond lapses (the record shows `unbound`), and the conditional backgrounding door needed `Prop_NoEndingAgainstRefusal`.

**Limits travel with the table.** G14 is a guarantee about the other thirteen: what they do not promise. Any surface that shows one guarantee must show G14.

**What a member who cannot leave is owed (CC 3.2 "Forbidding a room: no new power", CC 1.13.3.4, CC 5.4.6; CIRISEdge `docs/LIGHTNET_DARKNET.md`, `FSD/FIRST_CONTACT.md` §2.1).** The CC admits no group ban, by construction (`formal/mesh_net` `Prop_NoBan`), and refuses client-side scanning as the surveillance backdoor. What applies instead when a community keeps harm: its keepers are named by their own signed act; their keys are subject to `slash` and the key-scoped `quarantine` marker, which never withholds itself; trust is what admission composes over, so the honest mesh stops admitting, replicating for, and granting keys to them; a root may restrict what its own subscribers carry (relieve-never-expand); every owner chooses what their nodes hold; the harmed contest on the record and leave with standing intact; a child keeps the CC 3.4.13 floor in any community. The residue is the keepers' own devices holding each other's codes on the derived plane, which no network that no one owns can prevent, Tor included, with the public-door tripwire at every scope-widening promotion. The two powers that would reach further are refused because the hand that removes predators from everyone is the hand that removes dissidents, and a capability that provably does not exist cannot be seized or legally compelled (CIRISServer `FSD/PRIOR_ART.md`).

**Who holds a root (CC 4.2.3, 4.2.1, 4.2.2).** The shipped root is held by named humans with hardware-attested keys, not a company, on the public record; the roster is being widened toward about seven seats by roster change under the standing-majority rule. Founding a root takes a charter and named human holders; a node chooses its roots and the CIRIS root is only the default. The admitted limit: a vendor can pin a device to a root it holds; the answer is visibility, named holders, public acts, and one-step re-rooting (CIRISServer `FSD/PRIOR_ART.md`: pluggable roots are "the escape valve").

**Reviewer standing.** A reviewer of this piece may kill a row on either of two grounds: the row is not backed (no witness and no basis, and no ticket that will produce one), or the row is not universal (it names a substrate where it should name kind or stewardship).
