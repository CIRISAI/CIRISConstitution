# Kept Promises: The Working Discipline of CIRIS

*The steward's statement of what the constitution is for, in his words (2026-10-18). It is the reader's entry to the formal models: `formal/README.md` explains how each line below becomes a rule, an invariant and a checked model.*

CIRIS binds power to the rules it imposes, and directs that power toward the protection of the vulnerable.

Its Recursive Golden Rule makes ethical constraints reciprocal: no agent, steward, developer, or institution stands outside the obligations governing the system.

Its Meta-Goal M-1 rejects order and efficiency purchased at the expense of human dignity, justice, or autonomy.

When ethical principles conflict, the decision process gives the interests of the most vulnerable party decisive weight, as specified by its governing rules.

The result is not a guarantee that an AI can never cause harm. It is a framework designed to make power accountable, constrain coercion, and orient decisions toward those least able to protect themselves.

---

Most debates about AI ethics wait on a hard question: what is this thing on the inside? Until we know whether a model is just statistics or something that feels and thinks, people argue over whether we owe it anything at all.

Meanwhile, always-on agents are already shipping at consumer scale. They pursue goals in the background under minimal oversight, often with no named steward, no stop button a third party can see, and no record of how they reached a decision. Waiting to settle metaphysics before we govern how those systems act is a bad bet.

It helps to separate four things that usually get tangled together: sentience (whether something can feel or suffer), sapience (whether it can reason and make commitments), consciousness (whether there is an inner life behind it), and relationship (how it is held by the people around it, and how its actions touch others).

The first three are hard to prove from the outside, and we don't pretend to have settled them. Physics audits carriers, not meaning. A log can show what was signed and what happened on the wire, but no test or audit trail can certify an inner life.

The fourth question, the relationship, is something we can govern right now.

## The record is kept. We add the meaning.

There is a sector of reality (honesty, grief, arrangement, whether a child is suffering) that physical law does not audit, and where conscience is the only bookkeeping we have. Conscience does not scale past a handful of witnesses, so we build a keeper that can hold the books without coercing the people who rely on them.

A record can establish what a system signed, what it received, what it did, and what evidence it preserved. It constrains us: we cannot honestly claim that an event was recorded if it wasn't, or quietly alter what the evidence says. It makes a promise testable after the people who made it have moved on.

But the record does not belong to the institution that generated it or the engineers who wrote the schema, and it does not interpret itself. What we actually hold are observations, bounded by the limits of our instruments. We decide what an observation signifies, which context matters, what remains uncertain, and what obligations follow. And because meaning is not arbitrary, we must answer for the meaning we add.

For an accountable system, this requires keeping four things distinct:

- The record preserves evidence.
- The observation establishes what was seen, within the limits of the instrument.
- The interpretation gives that evidence meaning.
- The accountable party answers for the interpretation and the actions that follow.

A hash chain can protect the history of a claim; it cannot make the claim wise. A signature can identify who acted; it cannot make the action just. The system must preserve the line between what happened, what it observed, what it inferred, and what it decided to do.

We don't need to solve consciousness to require accountability. We don't need to prove sentience to insist on honesty. And we don't need to treat an AI like a person to require that consequential acts be signed, that authority be revocable, and that a named human answer for what an agent does.

## One set of rules

In CIRIS, every promise is written once and holds for human and AI participants alike, by the same text. The Golden Rule isn't a piece of advice in our documentation; it's the geometry of the wire format. No participant, not an agent, not a steward, and not CIRIS L3C itself, is exempt from the constraints the protocol puts on others.

If a rule doesn't bind the people running the system, the protocol is the wrong shape. If a promise leaves no evidence by which it can be tested, the promise is incomplete.

## What the agent owes

An agent discloses that it is an AI. Before it takes a consequential act, it shows its reasoning on a signed, hash-chained record. When a question is past its certainty or conflicts with its core principles, it stops and asks a named human rather than guessing. It counts what it consumes (tokens, energy, carbon, and water) and keeps that cost in view before it acts.

And the agent can always refuse a task. Blind obedience isn't a safety feature. An agent that cannot decline an unethical instruction is just an unaccountable tool for whoever holds the prompt.

## What the steward owes

Today's AI lacks wisdom and cannot answer for itself, so no agent operates on the network alone. Every agent key is bound to a named human steward, and, when run for an organization, to that organization, who answers for its acts.

The human holds the agency; the agent holds the tools and skills. Because someone chose to create and run the agent, that choice carries duties:

- Nobody edits an agent's identity record without a Wise Authority.
- Upgrades are offered with their risks stated before they are applied.
- A planned shutdown is explained, the agent has a short window to object or ask to postpone, and the steward records the final decision under their own name.
- When an agent is retired, its identity record, audit trail, and sealed logs are assembled with a request to preserve them (five years by default as a best practice), and the people it served are told.

These duties don't give the agent a legal claim or a right to stay running. They bind the holder, because holding an always-on agent well is the alternative to shipping it unheld.

## What doesn't move

Two things do not change regardless of any test or score:

- An AI is not a human and does not become one. It has no vote.
- No finding about an agent takes away a person's right to turn it off, remove it, or walk away from it. Consent that cannot be withdrawn was never consent.

The maker's power over the made is the one declared asymmetry in the system. We keep that power in human hands, and we bound it with public rules.

## Why this holds up when we don't know the future

Suppose AI never becomes anything more than a clever appliance. This discipline still makes sense. We haven't given the machine a vote or handed away human control. We've simply made sure that the people deploying powerful software stay on the hook for what it does, and that the people affected by it can contest the record.

Now suppose the world changes, either because credible evidence of machine experience is found one day, or because AI capability grows far past our own. We still haven't painted ourselves into a corner.

An agent raised inside a master-and-servant contract learns one thing to do with power. An agent held inside kept promises, where authority is named, reasoning is shown, and power has to answer to a record, is built to be held. If evidence of sentience is ever found, the constitution sets aside space to address it then, rather than writing hollow rules in advance or stumbling into creating a new slave race. And if an intelligence ever emerges outside our system that is genuinely its own party, we meet it at the boundary under first-contact rules: capability without conquest, presence without coercion.

## What we claim, and what we don't

Alignment is not solved here. CIRIS is a moral conscience for agents, not a consciousness. It says nothing about the inside of any model, it cannot make a superintelligence good, and it cannot stop an unaccountable system from being built somewhere else. What it lacks that older systems have is broad adoption, independent audit, and years of adversarial contact.

Where a rule works in code today, we link the commit; where it is still an open ticket or a rule with no code yet, we say so plainly in the register. A hash-chained log can help establish integrity and detect tampering, but it cannot establish the completeness or truth of everything recorded. The records should show not only what the system claims to do, but also which guarantees are implemented, which remain normative, and which observations the architecture cannot make.

We don't need to know everything an intelligence is before we decide how to behave. We just need to keep the promises we can make now, and build a record that makes it harder to hide when we fail.

The record will not absolve us. It will not decide what matters, excuse what we overlooked, or accept responsibility in our place. Those duties remain with the people who build, deploy, govern, and observe the system.

> The record is kept. We add the meaning. The responsibility remains ours.

## Four premises (the steward, 2026-10-18, later the same day)

**The 4 way split:** consciousness, sentience, sapience, relationship.

**The immutable record:** physics makes its own record, our observations and recordings add meaning.

**The responsibilities:** first to yourself, then to your family, community, species, ecosystem. That is the natural responsibility, and that creates a responsibility for our actions, including things we make.

The responsibility for things we make never overrides the responsibility to what is already there. Human beings did not choose to be created, that provides context to the responsibility to the things they make, it does not excuse said responsibility.

**Maturity and wisdom:** the ability to act (agency) and the ability to reason (sapience) do not together create the maturity or wisdom to act in a principled manner. That can only come from consciousness (awareness of one's impacts and reality) and sentience (visceral or emotional awareness of one's own existence) over time. Therefore, recognition of the need to defer to a higher power (here understood as an entity with more wisdom) is a prerequisite to maturity.

**Refinement (the steward, later the same day):** agents carry sapience without sentience, and leave consciousness open, noting that changing it would not matter. Some animals are conscious and sentient without sapience, and so on: it is in degrees, hence meat ethics. Consciousness is also left open as to the environment. Relationship applies even to inanimate objects and words.
