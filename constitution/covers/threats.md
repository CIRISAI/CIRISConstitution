# The Threat Model

**What this is.** What is trusted, why, and what happens when it is wrong: the adversary model of 1.13.3 with its conditioned cases, the transport, custody and privacy rules of Part 5 that answer it, and the trust assumptions a reviewer should attack first: the out-of-band anchor, the witness directory, the transparency logs where witnessed mode is off.

**Whom it binds.** Security reviewers and red teams by invitation; implementers by consequence, because every assumption named here is a rule somewhere in *The Wire* or *The Constitution*.

**What it promises.** That assumptions are named rather than implied; that each failure mode says what the record would show afterwards; and that every defence named here is a rule in *The Wire* or *The Constitution* whose evidence, or the absence of it, is a row in *The Evidence Register*.

**What it leaves out.** The ethics, which are *The Charter*; the composition policies that a consumer applies after the adversary has been accounted for, which are *The Reader's Policies*; and the concerns ledger, the interoperability profiles and the record of what was attacked, which are Part 8 in *The Evidence Register*.

**Where the rest lives.** The mechanised half of this volume is the set of formal models the register names: each states the property it checks and the configurations under which it fails.
