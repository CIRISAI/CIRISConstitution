# The Charter (ciris_constitution-1.0-rc12-charter.pdf, 39 pages)

**Care score:** 4  (1 = ship as is, 5 = needs a rewrite pass before 1.0)
**One-line verdict:** The adoption/conformance split and the no-trade rule are usable, but the volume then ranks, optimises, balances, and legislates wire behavior in the same breath, in draft voice, with unsourced numbers.

## Strengths
- Adoption is separated from wire conformance on the first page and again at the force rule: a lethal deployment “has not adopted this charter, whatever its wire conformance says” (p. 1, p. 29–30).
- The no-trade rule is stated as a constraint, not a weight, and the Order-Maximisation Veto is defined as deferral because the quantities “share no unit” (p. 4–5).
- Turning an agent off is repeatedly distinguished from the Retirement Standard owed by whoever retires a deployed artefact (p. 12, p. 34).
- A0–A4 are explicitly disambiguated from substrate rungs A0..A5 (p. 31).

## Weaknesses
- Step 5 still says “Implement the ethically optimal action” (p. 5) after p. 4 has said the principles “are constraints, not weights” and that M-1 “is not a quantity to maximise.” Step 3 tells the agent to “Articulate principle conflicts or trade-offs” (p. 5); p. 8 evaluates “trade-offs”; creation says the principles “must be balanced” (p. 25); the recursive fail-safe is “locally optimised” (p. 22).
- p. 4 says a precondition is “prior to other claims rather than weightier than them.” p. 11 says “a single child has more moral standing than every datacenter in the world.”
- p. 1: charter duties are “plain imperatives, not in RFC 2119 terms,” and a capital MUST “quotes a rule whose home is The Wire or The Constitution.” p. 6 and p. 14–15 state original MUSTs (“The substrate MUST NOT be relied on”, “the substrate MUST purge it”, “MUST emit a terminal ACTION_RESULT”) with no home section quoted.
- Decommissioning “proceeds through six ordered stages” (p. 31), then the same page says the notice periods, Sunset PDMA, hardware disposal, contracts, and review “is conduct a steward chooses and records.”
- “Failures to meet the duties outlined in this section may constitute grounds for a claim,” and claims are “often referred to as ‘Creator Negligence Claims’ (CNCs)” (p. 28–29). p. 29 then says “CIRIS creates no claim where the law recognises none.” p. 35 adds an “Improper Sunset Claim” docket and “This document creates no legal obligation.”
- History and process notes sit in the Parts: “as of this writing,” an unnamed product “shipped the day after its vendor withheld a successor model,” sources parked at CC 8.6.1, which this volume does not contain (p. 12–13; Part 8 here starts at 8.7). “No such act has occurred,” “none has been found” (p. 12). “Deferred pending a wire-level design” (p. 17). “We have not defined the class… honestly, near-unfalsifiable” and “Adversarial reviewers: this is the test to push on” (p. 6–7). “The enumeration lives in the canonical working draft” (p. 7).
- Comparative or analogical claims: “Cartesian-individualist default… in cryptographic identity work” (p. 10); tiers “modeled on SAE J3016” and DoD 3000.09, with EU AI Act Article 14 as “the general-purpose legal anchor” (p. 31); RM bands tied to MIL-STD-882E, DO-178C, ARP4761A, and the EU AI Act (p. 26); “a benevolent-steward model in the lineage of the open-source projects this ultimately is” (p. 39); MCAS as what “CIRIS Would Require” (p. 36).
- Numbers in prose with no source in this volume: 100 000 MAU and 180 days (p. 5); 0.1 % operations levy (p. 16); > 0.5 % metric change (p. 22); audiences > 10,000, harm-uplift ≥ 0.5 %, opacity > 80 % “NIST guidelines or similar standards” (p. 27–28); 50,000 people and 30 days (p. 28); KPI ≥ 20 % for three quarters, ≥ 100 k users (p. 31); ≥ 90 days and > 50 k users, 120 days (p. 32); five years, ≥ 30 days, 5 % “avowedly arbitrary,” 259 200 s (p. 32–34); ≥ 100,000 production agents in 24 hours (p. 39). The 100,000 on p. 5 and the 100,000 on p. 39 are different gates.
- “Six Core Principles defined in CC 1.4–1.12” (p. 19) points at a range that includes the kill-switch (1.5), the 1+4 essay (1.7), and WBD (1.9). The list of record is at 1.15.1 (p. 16). 1.14 is missing and, unlike 1.2 and 1.13.3, not marked as printed elsewhere (p. 4, p. 13, p. 15).
- 1.15.10 Conclusion precedes 1.15.11 Introduction (p. 18). 1.16.1 Conclusion precedes the section it concludes (p. 19). The WBD trigger list and deferral procedure on p. 19 repeat p. 8–9 verbatim. “plain rule 7b” (p. 21), “plain rule N5,” “plain rules 1c and 1e,” and “G16” (p. 32–34) do not resolve here. “(formal/retirement)” (p. 34) reads as a leftover tag.
- Case-study lesson: “Integrity sometimes means prioritising the law’s purpose over its letter” (p. 38), against the no-trade rule and against “a mandate is not exceeded” (p. 21). “May the 346 lost lives anchor our commitment” (p. 36) is memorial rhetoric. Closing line “Soli Deo Gloria” (p. 39) is a sectarian colophon on a text that says it speaks to the public (p. 1).
- The foreword is myth, not a rule (“children of starlight,” “The lullaby fades here,” p. 2–3). That may be the promised narrative, but it is not labelled as non-operative the way the later “CIRIS editorial” notes are (p. 4, p. 17).

## Opportunities
- One substitution pass on “optimal,” “trade-off,” “balanced,” “weightier,” and “locally optimised,” using only the fail-safe sentence already in step 4 (p. 5), removes the worst self-contradiction without a new rule.
- Mark every capital MUST as a quotation plus a Wire or Constitution section, or rewrite it as a plain imperative. The ACTION_RESULT and retirement-family paragraphs (p. 14–15, p. 32–34) are wire text in the wrong volume.
- Open 7.5.4 with a single sentence: which of the six stages are duties, and which are recorded choices. Delete whichever of “proceeds through six ordered stages” or “conduct a steward chooses” is the loser (p. 31).
- Delete CNC, ISC, and the remedy catalogue, and keep the jurisdictional sentence (p. 29, p. 35). The legal posture is already written; the docket names undo it.
- Either print the CC 8.6.1 sources in this volume or delete the unnamed-vendor sentence (p. 12–13). A duty grounded on a fact the reader cannot see will be attacked first.

## Threats
- A lawyer will quote “grounds for a claim” and “Creator Negligence Claims” (p. 28–29) against “creates no claim” and “creates no legal obligation” (p. 29, p. 35), and will treat the Wise Authority / Wisdom Bank apparatus as a private court despite the disclaimer.
- An implementer will not know whether failing to purge in-flight traces, emit ACTION_RESULT, or mint `retirement:*` rows (p. 14–15, p. 32–34) is a conformance failure. p. 1 says they may ignore this volume and still conform.
- A regulator will treat the 5 % sentience figure, the 0.5 % harm uplift, the 80 % opacity line, and the 0.1 % levy as invented precision. The 5 % sentence admits no standard derives it (p. 33–34); the opacity sentence cites “NIST guidelines or similar” without a document (p. 28). The product-shipment premise (p. 12–13) is unverifiable from here, not shown to be invented.
- p. 39 lets “the founder, or by any accord-holder” amend the text, then says “the maintainer decides,” then defers founder authority until a scale gate. p. 9–10 say no principal, “including the steward,” is exempt. A hostile reader will call the ethics founder-editable.
- Case study 6 (p. 37–38) teaches overriding a literal safety rule by modelled consequence. That is the comparison the Veto on p. 5 exists to stop. Case study 5 (p. 37–38) has a surveillance system and “counter-measures” with no mention of the lethal-action rule on p. 29–30.
- “not to create a new slave race” (p. 12, p. 33), beside an arbitrary 5 % trigger, is the sentence a hostile reviewer will excerpt.
