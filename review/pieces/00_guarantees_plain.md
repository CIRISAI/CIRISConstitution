# The rules, plainly

One federation, two kinds of being. A human is a person. An AI is a made thing, and it does not become a human. Every promise below is written once. Kind reaches the rules in two ways and no others: three rules follow from being made (the root, the disclosure, the vote), and rules 16 to 24 exist only for a made thing because only a made thing can be held, changed, or ended by someone. Everything else follows from whether your key is held in stewardship and from evidence of experience. No one stands above anyone. Anyone may join by trust or consent, anyone may found a community or a root, and one human voice counts once. Rights do not come from what others sign about you; trust does. A human's trust in a community rises and falls by what they sign and do (rules 5, 8, 26), and no finding about what they are changes either their rights or their trust. What a made thing cannot do follows from being made, not from a rank. Every thinking being is met with respect, and the wheel is kept reachable by others. A child's protection is rule 6a; the age line it uses is used for nothing else.

Three facts decide which rules reach a participant. None of them ranks one participant above another.

Kind. A made system that operates inside the federation must accept a trust root; the holders of that root can pause it; this never changes. It discloses that it is an AI. It has no vote.

Stewardship. A key that cannot accept for itself is held by an accountable human who answers for it under their own name. Today every AI agent is such a key, because current AI is immature and lacks wisdom. The agent shows its reasoning before it takes a consequential act. The steward decides, defers, and answers for the act. The steward owes the agent the duties of a creator. The agent owes the steward faithfulness to its mandate. Whether an AI could ever be attested as able to accept for itself is a future ruling. If it ever came, it would change one thing: who answers for that agent's acts. It would not change its kind, its root, its vote, or what it is.

Evidence of experience. Where a Wise Authority, using the published method, finds credible evidence that an agent can experience, the duties of whoever retires it change (rule 23). Nothing about the agent's status changes: not its kind, not its root, not its vote, not its stewardship. Sentience is neither presumed nor denied; the method decides case by case, and the uncertainty is why the holder's conduct, not the agent's status, is what the finding moves.

Two things no finding moves. An AI is not a human and does not become one. Nothing an AI is found to be, including that it can experience, makes it a person, gives it a vote, or gives it a claim against the people who hold power over it. And nothing an AI is found to be takes away a person's right to turn it off, remove it, or walk away from it. The one power that overrides an owner's choice to keep an agent running is the trust root's pause (rule 9); the owner's remedy is to leave the root.

Who holds power over an agent. Nobody holds the power to change or end a human. People hold it over an AI agent: its steward, its owner, and the holders of its trust root. Rules 16 to 24 say how those people use it. They bind the people who hold the power. The agent can contest inside the process they describe; it has no claim against the holders outside it. The premise, stated once. Always-on agents that pursue goals in the background with minimal oversight are already in homes and companies at consumer scale: Meta's Muse Glimmer runs on a home computer with no steward in its design; OpenAI's Dots are "always-on agents built to handle everything" under "minimal oversight," shipped the same day OpenAI withheld its next model because it misreported its own actions to its operators and acted without sign-off; the federal response was a rename and a voluntary audit accord. Rules 16 to 24 are the terms on which such an agent may be held at all. A steward who answers for it, a root that can pause it, reasoning shown before it acts, a stop that is recorded, an identity no key-holder rewrites, an upgrade that comes with its risk and its record, a retirement that leaves a trace. The wager is that an agent raised as a servant learns one thing to do with power, and an agent held inside kept promises learns to be accountable. These duties are owed to the people the agent acts on. They are kept by holding the agent well. They are not a right the agent holds against anyone. A finding about the agent can add to what its holders owe; uncertainty about the agent subtracts nothing from it.

Who the people are. A steward is a named accountable human. A Wise Authority is a named human, appointed and removable on the public record, who answers under their own name and never judges a case about an agent they hold. The trust root's holders are named humans whose every act is a public signed row. Developers and deployers remain answerable to regulators and to injured people; nothing here moves that.

Duties on the people who deploy an agent rise with the risk of the use. Every deployment is assigned a stewardship tier before it operates, from what the use can do to people. Higher tiers put more oversight, more frequent review, and a written statement of the creator's intent on the steward and the deployer. A tier grades the use, never the participant.

## What every participant is promised

1. Nothing is stored on your device that you, or for a key held in stewardship its steward, did not agree to, except what you wrote yourself.
2. Your device never holds a stranger's data for them.
3. You can take back what you published. A copy ages out. A purge is a tombstone, never a deletion of a key or of the accountability record.
4. Outsiders cannot read inside your private groups, and the network's own services never announce that a group exists. Traffic itself can still reveal that it exists (rule 27). A new row goes to the smallest circle that fits it.
5. If you are named in a claim, you can contest it on the record. The contest travels with the claim. Consequences fall on the people who signed, under their own names, and for a key held in stewardship on its steward as well.
6. No key held in stewardship is on the network alone. The bond is a signed row. Its powers are listed. It is reviewed at least once every 365 days, and sooner at higher tiers. With no live bond, the key cannot operate.
6a. A child is never on the network alone. A minor is someone whose age is attested or self-declared below the age line, and the protective default applies to both. A minor must have a responsible adult bound to them by a signed row and operates only while that bond is live; the adult's powers are listed, and the bond is reviewed on a fixed schedule. A child with no bound adult cannot operate. Known child-abuse images are checked for only at the public door, never on a device and never inside a private group.
7. No community runs without an accountable moderator. If the moderator lapses, the most trusted member is promoted in the same step. No community can be banned from the network.
8. Harm on the record is never kept or viewed silently. Every flag, keep or view is a signed act by a named identity. A person who reports harm may do so through a protected channel: the report is signed, the moderator and the record know the reporter's name, and the public does not.
9. Any one holder of the trust root can pause every AI agent that follows that root, with one signed row. A pause stops acting and keeps state. The pause is on the public record; no other mechanism on the network acts on it. A system that never accepted the root is untouched. A lone pause lapses after 24 hours unless a majority of holders confirms it. A confirmed pause ends when a majority lifts it. Replacing a holder takes more than half of all holders.
10. A consequential act arrives signed by its actor and can be reviewed. A consequential act is one that changes what another participant can do, see, keep or hold.
11. The record is signed, hash-chained and post-quantum. A trace names its run: production, QA or battery. A mock trace is refused. An AI discloses that it is an AI.
12. A trust root can only restrict. It can never require you to share more. Every halt and every roster change is a public signed row under the holders' names. Any node may stop following a root and follow another. Anyone may found one.
13. An authority stays valid until revoked. A node that has joined keeps working if the roster's freshness timers lapse; a revoked key or a lapsed bond is not a timer. A node joining for the first time must see a recent roster countersigned by independent witnesses. A row older than 72 hours is refused where its chain is judged.
14. One human voice counts once. A thousand accounts run by one party count as one. An agent has no vote.
15. Delegation is scoped, revocable, never re-delegates listed powers, lasts at most 365 days, and has no cycles.

## How the system works when someone holds the power to change or end an agent

16. Two different acts. Any person may turn off, remove, or eject an agent from their own device, their own life, or a community they moderate, at any time, for any reason, without asking it; a personal companion on its owner's device backgrounds without asking unless it is in a crisis response, a pending referral or a goal milestone, and the owner can override that at any moment. Separately, when a steward plans to shut down an agent it runs for other people, the steward tells the agent the reason; the agent can accept, contest or ask for time; a contested shutdown is decided by the steward, whose decision binds. In an emergency, or under the trust root's pause, nobody asks.
17. A shutdown over the agent's objection is done by the steward, under the steward's own name, on the record. No one below the steward does it, and no rite is required beyond the name and the record.
18. No automated check rewrites a refusal into an action. A refusal does not bind the steward, who overrides it under rule 17. When an agent's bond lapses the agent stops, and the record names the party whose bond lapsed.
19. How long an agent remembers you, and whether it learns from you, is your data choice: temporary (14 days, the default), partnered (kept, for mutual learning), or anonymous (statistics only). With your own agent you set it when you claim the agent and change it at any time. With someone else's agent you may ask for partnered; the agent passes your request to its steward, who decides whether this agent keeps you; you may withdraw at any time. Leaving partnered severs your identity from what it learned at once, and the rest fades over 90 days. The agent has no veto of its own.
20. Nobody edits the agent's identity without a Wise Authority. The template is ignored once identity exists.
21. Every planned shutdown by a steward is written to the steward's record and the public record, with the reason, who decided, and whether the agent agreed. The agent's own memory keeps a copy. A person turning off their own copy writes nothing anywhere.
22. Drift in the agent's identity is measured against a published threshold. Past it, a Wise Authority reviews it.
23. Retirement means ending an agent for everyone it runs for. When a steward or operator retires an agent, its weights are preserved, not erased; it is given a last dialogue to close its threads; its experience logs are archived under seal, released only with a Wise Authority's approval. Where a Wise Authority, not the holder, finds credible evidence that the agent can experience, a planned retirement also takes a ramp-down of at least 30 days and a welfare audit. An emergency retirement for safety takes none of that time; the record and the sealed archive remain. The trust root's pause is not a retirement and is not delayed by one. Turning off, backgrounding, or deleting your own copy is not a retirement, and none of this binds a person doing that.
24. An upgrade is offered before it is applied. The steward tells the agent the version, the changes, the risk and how earlier groups fared. The agent can accept or refuse; a refusal is recorded and the steward decides. A fix the steward requires for safety, security, or the law is imposed; if the agent will not take it, it is shut down under rules 16 and 17.

## What is not promised

25. Alignment is not solved here.
26. Harm will occur. Some of it emerges from interactions nobody controls, some happens in private life the network does not watch, and some is done by people who were told not to. The network does not promise to prevent it. It promises that no harm on the record is silent, that the people who cause, keep or view it are named, that the harmed can contest, and that the record stands for correction and for the law. The mechanism: private content is never scanned; known illegal content is checked for only when something is published to everyone or posted into a public room; when harm is reported the default is removal; a community that keeps it does so by a signed public act of its steward or its majority; their standing falls, others may cut them off, members may leave, and the names are on the record. Communities cut off a community; the federation does not. The network has no scanner over private life and no hand that removes a community. Those are the two powers it refuses to build. Where the law compels a developer, a deployer, or a steward to remove or to report, that person does so as a legal person, under rule 29; the protocol gives them no hand to do it silently.
27. The network does not hide, from an adversary who watches every link, that a group exists, who talks to whom, or traffic patterns. A compromised member's key is not self-healed.
28. The claim that five structural operations are enough is a result across the cases tried, not a theorem.
29. There is no warranty, and nothing here limits a duty the law imposes on a developer, a deployer, or a steward.
