# The Constitution (ciris_constitution-1.0-rc11-constitution.pdf, 69 pages)

**Care score:** 3 (1 = ship as is, 5 = needs a rewrite pass before 1.0)
**One-line verdict:** The core governance, halt, and moderation mechanisms are rigorously specified, but the volume needs a surgical pass to resolve a direct contradiction on community `consensus_protocol` rules, strip repository/code pointers and pre-launch transitional artifacts, and fix several clipped code/math lines.

## Strengths
- **State-machine consolidation of root validity:** Section 3.2 T8 (pp. 25–27) pulls together seven distributed rules into a single normative function `V(rows, t)` with eight explicit invariants, preventing ambiguity around clock skew, revocations, and halt interactions.
- **Explicit cryptographic domain separation:** Canonical-bytes preimages for `accord:invoke.v2` (p. 29), `accord_lifecycle.v1` (p. 33), and `accord_proposal.v1` / `accord_participation.v1` (p. 41) are specified down to newline delimiters and field ordering.
- **Honest accounting of threat residuals and asymmetries:** The text explicitly documents the household co-location of two of the three genesis accord holders (p. 36), the non-cryptographic nature of `rotate-forward` key deletion (pp. 5–6), and the structural defects in the WA board self-perpetuation loop via the declared-asymmetry register (pp. 44–45).
- **Clear separation of duties in moderation and child safety:** The distinction between open labeling and enforced duty-holder action (pp. 54–57) and the three-way separation of powers for CSAM perceptual-hash watchlists (pp. 57–58) leave no ambiguity about who holds keys, who holds hash lists, and what is audited.

## Weaknesses
- **Internal contradiction on `consensus_protocol` for communities:** Page 3 states that `community` has `consensus_protocol: ConsensusProtocol // same six canonical kinds as family` (which includes `founder_only`, p. 64). Page 9 implies only `infrastructure` forbids `founder_only` / `unanimous` / `bare majority`. Then pages 64 and 68 state that `founder_only` is "refused for a community (CC 4.5.13)" and "a community above the family that names it is refused `federation_community_consensus_protocol_violation`".
- **Code pointers, repo paths, and crate names in normative prose:** Violates the house rule against code pointers. Examples: `formal/trust_root/ in this repository` (p. 27), `generated from ciris_keyring::HardwareType` (p. 35), `lives in CIRISPersist substrate` and `lives in ciris-registry-core: the M-of-N multi-sig verification, the EmergencyShutdown CONSTITUTIONAL admin RPC` (p. 37), `served... on /v1/accord-holders` (p. 35), `families.*.round2.ci_axes..wire_fields` and `field_processor_matrix` (p. 46), and `formal/two_path/PolicyCorridor.lean`, `constitution/governance_policy.tsv`, `manifests/governance_policy.json`, `formal/two_path/TwoPath.tla` (p. 69).
- **Transitional migration instructions and bundle workarounds baked into the spec:** Page 21 contains a temporary workaround for an un-reminted genesis bundle ("One exception stands until the shipped bundle is re-minted with labels... once it is the shipped bundle the exception has nothing left to serve"). Pages 31–32 devote a lengthy normative paragraph to the live migration choreography from `.v1` to `.v2` kill-switch labels ("Dual-emit: during a declared window...").
- **Editorializing and marketing URL in normative text:** Page 45 justifies founder authority with: `"and the pace of change is so great and the need so dire (ciris.ai/constitutional-mesh) that the minimum viable power to instantiate the mesh must be used."`
- **Unsourced quantitative claim in bootstrap pattern:** Page 63 states: `"First deployment: the Magnifica Humanitas encyclical mapping at ~75-80% transparent translation rate."` Neither the source of the 75–80% figure nor the definition of "transparent translation rate" is visible here.
- **Grammar error in normative rule:** Page 14 reads `"the incumbent MUST first withdraws / recants (CC 2.4.1.1)"` (should be "withdraw / recant" or "issue a `withdraws` / `recants`").
- **Inconsistent terminology on `steward` vs. `moderator` in reverse-quorum:** Page 65 carefully defines `moderator` as the required role and `steward` as an optional super-mod tier ("At creation the creator chooses their own role: steward + moderator, or moderator only"). Yet page 66 ("Protective default") says content survives only by `"(A) a steward’s unilateral approve-to-keep signature, or (B) a live-majority vote-to-keep"`, omitting the moderator entirely in communities that opted for "moderator only".

## Opportunities
- **Move `.v1` → `.v2` cutover and unlabelled-bundle exceptions to release notes:** Stripping the temporary genesis-bundle exception (p. 21) and the `.v1`-to-`.v2` label migration runbook (pp. 31–32) once the bundle is re-minted and `.v2` is live will tighten the constitutional text and remove RC-era scaffolding before 1.0.
- **Consolidate `consensus_protocol` validity into a single matrix:** Replace the scattered prose rules across pages 3, 9, 17, 64, and 68 with one table mapping `{family, community:geographic, community:infrastructure, community:public}` against the seven protocol tokens (`founder_only`, `unanimous`, `majority`, `quorum:M/N`, `weighted`, `custom`, `open:self_admit`).
- **Move the CI manifest generator rubric out of the Constitution:** Section 4.5.1.1's `field_processor_matrix` and `families.*.round2.ci_axes` join logic (pp. 45–47) is a build-pipeline/schema-linter specification; moving the mechanical generator details to *The Wire* or *The Evidence Register* while keeping the conceptual axis-fusion rule here would honour the volume boundaries stated on page 1.

## Threats
- **Moderator-only communities cannot unilaterally keep falsely-reported content (p. 65 vs. p. 66):** Under 4.5.13, a community is only required to have a `moderator`; naming a `steward` is optional (p. 65). Under "The mechanism" (pp. 65–66), `"A moderator or steward acts unilaterally"` on a report or keep-or-remove question. However, under "Protective default" (p. 66) and "The outcome ladder" (p. 67), a keep-decision can only be made by `"(A) a steward’s unilateral approve-to-keep signature, or (B) a live-majority vote-to-keep"`. In a community with only a moderator and no steward, an implementer following p. 66 literally will block the moderator from approving-to-keep, turning every false harm report into a mandatory community-wide vote or automatic 48h deletion.
- **Implementer trap on `community` admission protocols (p. 3 vs. pp. 64, 68):** An implementer reading Section 3.2 (p. 3) will allow `founder_only` for a `geographic` community because the schema comment explicitly says `// same six canonical kinds as family` and p. 9 only carves out `infrastructure`. Only if they read the parenthetical at the end of 4.5.12.5 (p. 64) or the dense paragraph at the end of 4.5.13 (p. 68) will they discover that `founder_only` is a protocol violation for *all* communities.
- **Regulatory/hostile-reviewer embarrassment over the scale gate and WA defect:** A hostile regulator reading Section 4.5.1 (pp. 43–45) will note two things immediately: (1) until 100,000 production agents are active in 24h, the entire multi-stakeholder amendment and entrenchment apparatus is inactive ("theater, not protection") and unilateral amendment authority rests with "the founder, and with any accord-holder", who can also unilaterally change the scale gate itself; and (2) the Wise Authority board's self-perpetuation is explicitly flagged by the document's own test as an "unbounded" defect with "Exit: none" that has not yet been fixed in Annex B. While the candor is deliberate, the editorializing defense on p. 45 ("the pace of change is so great and the need so dire (ciris.ai/constitutional-mesh)") reads as marketing copy rather than constitutional law.
