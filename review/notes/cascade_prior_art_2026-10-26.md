# Prior-art review of the cascade model (2026-10-26)

For the steward of the text. Files read, unchanged: `formal/reciprocity/Cascade.lean` (whole), `formal/reciprocity/README.md`, `formal/reciprocity/Reciprocity.lean` §1'', §10, §13, §14, §15, and the root `README.md` sections "In brief" and "The ethics cascade". Line numbers are as of commit 961a6da.

The most consequential finding concerns Reciprocity.lean §15, not Cascade.lean. `passesSwap` (Reciprocity.lean:612) is a status-quo Pareto test, not a swap test. Read literally, it forbids any rule that moves anything from the strong seat to the weak seat. Separately, Cascade's sentence 1 (Cascade.lean:99) never uses `passesSwap` or positions at all. It is a list of forbidden act features, and no theorem connects the two.

## 1. What the construction most resembles

### Direct resemblances (the same object)

- **Weak Pareto and individual rationality.** `acceptsFrom` and `passesSwap` (Reciprocity.lean:606–613) require that neither seat ends worse than it began. That is weak Pareto non-worsening from the status quo. In mechanism design it is the individual-rationality (participation) constraint, with the status quo as the outside option. `swap_is_symmetric` (Reciprocity.lean:637) is the anonymity axiom applied to that test.
- **Scanlon's individualist restriction.** Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other* (1998), ch. 5: principles are assessed by individuals' reasons, never by summed benefits. `efficiency_at_weak_expense_fails_swap` (Reciprocity.lean:616) is that restriction in two lines; its aggregate hypothesis `_hagg` is never used. Scanlon is the right citation here, not Rawls.
- **Hare's role reversal.** R. M. Hare, *Freedom and Reason* (1963), ch. 6, and *Moral Thinking* (1981): would you accept the prescription were you in the other's place. This is the nearest ancestor of the wording "acceptable to whoever would stand in the weaker one". Hare's argument ends in preference utilitarianism, the conclusion CIRIS rejects, so the citation must say where the paths part.
- **Relationship-based access control.** "Only the relationship is governed" is ReBAC: rules read relations (made-by, holds, granted, answered-for), never attributes of kind. See Fong, "Relationship-based access control" (CODASPY 2011), and Pang et al., "Zanzibar" (USENIX ATC 2019).
- **Separation of duty.** Occupancy (Cascade.lean:101: a made thing never `canAnswer`) is a static separation-of-duty constraint in RBAC: a made thing is never assigned the answering role. See Sandhu et al., RBAC96 (IEEE Computer, 1996); Ahn and Sandhu, RCL 2000 (ACM TISSEC, 2000); NIST RBAC (Ferraiolo et al., 2001).
- **Revocable capabilities and responsibility tracing.** Revocable grants are Redell's revocable forwarder, the caretaker pattern (Redell, 1974; Miller, *Robust Composition*, 2006). `answeredBy : Option Participant` (Cascade.lean:63) closely parallels Horton: Miller, Donnelley and Karp, "Delegating Responsibility in Digital Systems: Horton's 'Who Done It?'" (HotSec 2007).
- **LogiKEy.** The method of encoding a normative theory, proving what follows, and exhibiting small countermodels for what does not, is the LogiKEy methodology: Benzmüller, Parent and van der Torre, *Artificial Intelligence* 287 (2020), Isabelle/HOL with Nitpick. `bare` (Cascade.lean:319) plays the role of a Nitpick countermodel.
- **Assurance cases.** `standsOn` (Cascade.lean:279–290) is a goal structure. Promises are goals, the four sentences are the strategy, the `X_*` premises (Cascade.lean:135–148) are GSN Assumption nodes, and `bare` is a defeater. See the GSN Community Standard v3 (2021); Kelly's thesis (York, 1998); Bloomfield and Rushby, "Assurance 2.0" (2020); Goodenough, Weinstock and Klein, eliminative argumentation (ICSE 2013).

### Analogical resemblances (cite, the object differs)

- **Rawls.** "Relevant social positions" (*A Theory of Justice*, §16) and the veil of ignorance resemble "positions, not persons" and blindness to the inner three. In Rawls the parties choose under ignorance and arrive at maximin. Here nobody chooses, and the test is not maximin (see section 2).
- **Harsanyi's equiprobability.** Harsanyi (1953, 1955) puts the chooser in each seat with equal probability and derives average utility. Here each seat has one fixed representative, there is no lottery, and the aggregate is deliberately never read.
- **Leximin and maximin.** `weak_position_decides` (Reciprocity.lean:626) agrees with leximin only in the case its hypothesis `h2s` admits, where the strong seat is not lowered.
- **Envy-freeness.** The test is not an envy test. Envy compares my bundle with yours; `acceptsFrom` compares each seat with its own past.
- **Kant.** The contradiction-in-conception and contradiction-in-will tests ask whether a maxim survives universalisation. Nothing here tests for contradiction. The relabel theorems are closer to the formal-universality reading of Kant: a rule that names no particular.
- **Habermas.** Principle (U) (*Moral Consciousness and Communicative Action*, 1983): a norm is valid only if all affected can accept the consequences for each one's interests. That is close to sentence 1, but (U) and (D) require an actual discourse, and `contestable : Bool` (Cascade.lean:65) is the only stand-in.
- **Ostrom.** The promises can be parsed in the institutional grammar, Attributes, Deontic, aIm, Conditions, Or else (Crawford and Ostrom, APSR 89(3), 1995; IG 2.0, Frantz and Siddiki, 2022). They also match several design principles of *Governing the Commons* (1990): boundaries are adoption, monitoring is the record, conflict resolution is contest, recognition of the right to organise is the no-ban rule, nested enterprises are roots and federation.
- **Hart, Kelsen, Suber.** Hart's primary and secondary rules (*The Concept of Law*, 1961): sentence 1 is primary; sentence 4 is mostly a secondary rule of recognition and change. The basis as a whole plays the part of a basic norm. Suber, *The Paradox of Self-Amendment* (1990), and Nomic bear on R7 and the amendment power (§3 of Reciprocity.lean).
- **Governance specifications and Constitutional AI.** Tezos and Polkadot OpenGov formalise voting procedures; Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) is natural-language principles used in training. Neither shares the object. Closer prior art is normative multi-agent systems: electronic institutions (Esteva and Sierra, Islander), OperA (Dignum), and Boella and van der Torre's normative MAS work.

## 2. What each tradition would say is missing or wrong

- **`lowersWeak` assumes a welfare comparison it never makes.** It is a fixed list of act features (Cascade.lean:91–92), with no outcome function, no position and no strong/weak pair. "Each is something nobody accepts from the weaker seat" (Cascade.lean:88–90) is asserted, not derived. Logically these are Nozick-style side constraints, absolute and independent of who holds power. An unconsented touch on private content is forbidden even when the weaker party does it to the stronger, as in whistleblowing. A Scanlonian or Rawlsian would say Cascade's sentence 1 is not the swap test of §15. No bridge theorem shows that each `lowersWeak` act fails `passesSwap` under any map from acts to outcomes.
- **The swap test is status-quo Pareto, not maximin.** `acceptsFrom .strong` requires `after.strong ≥ before.strong` (Reciprocity.lean:607), so redistribution from strong to weak fails the test. In `weak_position_decides`, if the only resolution that protects the weak seat costs the strong seat, both resolutions fail and the tie-break says nothing. Rawls would call this the opposite of the difference principle. Harsanyi would call it an unargued refusal to aggregate. A mechanism designer would say it gives the strong seat a veto. The README's gloss "the weak position decides" (formal/reciprocity/README.md:52) says more than Reciprocity.lean:626 proves.
- **The weak seat is one agent, not a position over a distribution.** `Outcome` holds one `Int` for strong and one for weak. There is no set of least-advantaged parties, no distribution, no lottery. Who is weak is also fixed across before and after, although a change can reorder who is weak. Rawls uses a representative least-advantaged person defined by primary goods; Harsanyi takes an expectation over seats. The model does neither, and the README should say so.
- **"Positions, not persons" fails at three points.**
  - `made` is an origin fact, not an exchangeable position. The swap can never move anyone into or out of it. §1'' concedes this with `symmetry_silent_where_unoccupied` (Reciprocity.lean, §1''). A Rawlsian would conclude that the halt rests on R1, a status assignment, not on reciprocity.
  - `isHuman := !made` (Cascade.lean:47) brings kind back in. Any participant that is not made, including a node or an animal if one appeared in `ps`, satisfies "a named human answers". The role field goes unread only because `made` now carries the discrimination.
  - Reciprocity.lean reads the inner degrees. `hasStake` reads sentience (Reciprocity.lean:544), `isPrincipal` reads sapience (Reciprocity.lean:517), and grievance standing follows from them. `agent_verdict_independent_of_consciousness` varies consciousness, the one degree no function reads, while sentience is held at 0. The two files disagree on what a finding does. In Cascade it moves nothing (`finding_moves_no_verdict`, Cascade.lean:424). In Reciprocity §14, raising sentience creates a stake and so grievance standing. CC 7.5.5 sides with Cascade ("care is not standing, no finding changes the artefact's status", `constitution/part_7_lifecycle_stewardship.md:307`), so §14's `hasStake` is the definition to reconcile.
- **The blindness theorems are weaker than stated.** `relabel_keeps_the_facts` (Cascade.lean:370) holds by `rfl` because no definition mentions the fields. `verdicts_blind_to_inner_and_role` (Cascade.lean:410) is proved for the one family `bareWith`, not for all worlds. A noninterference theorist (Goguen and Meseguer, 1982) would ask for the universal statement: for every world, relabelling every participant leaves the basis and every promise unchanged.
- **The four-and-seven split is mostly definitional.**
  - `P10` is `X_measured` (Cascade.lean:257, proved by `hm`).
  - `P6` is `X_noBan ∧ X_adoption` (Cascade.lean:171, 228).
  - The first conjunct of `P7` is the flag `constraints = true` (Cascade.lean:173).
  - The four derived promises hold because `lowersWeak` and `Record` contain their negations almost verbatim.
  - The worked-backwards section adds `total_includes_cost` and `nobody_bound_who_did_not_join` to `Record'` (Cascade.lean:460–461) and three disjuncts to `lowersWeak'` (Cascade.lean:446–447). That moves promises into the basis by definition.

  An assurance-case reviewer would recognise assumptions relabelled as derived claims, which GSN treats as an unacknowledged assurance deficit. The honest name for the count is a classification of where each promise's content is written, not a derivation. The README sentence "four are consequences of the basis" (formal/reciprocity/README.md:227) needs that qualifier. The residue of a parameter, a disclaimer, a practice and one rule (`what_remains_outside`, Cascade.lean:546) is a known pattern: GSN separates Assumption, Justification and Context nodes in the same way. Cite it, do not present it as a finding.
- **Deontic logic would note what is absent.** There is no obligation operator, no conditional obligation, no contrary-to-duty structure. The remedy half of P8 is a textbook contrary-to-duty obligation, what is owed once harm has occurred (Chisholm's paradox, 1963). "Constraints, not weights" is a Bool in Cascade (Cascade.lean:85, 140), though §16 of Reciprocity.lean gives it content. Defeasible deontic logic (Governatori and Rotolo) and Catala's prioritised defaults (Merigoux, Chataing and Protzenko, ICFP 2021) are the established ways to state no-trade and lexical priority.
- **Deferral is a static flag.** `defersWhenUnsure : Bool` (Cascade.lean:40) models neither uncertainty nor the addressee of deferral. A mechanism designer would note that unsureness is self-reported, so the rule has no incentive-compatibility content.
- **Ostrom's grammar has no "or else".** Few of the eleven promises carry a sanction clause, and ADICO separates norms from rules exactly by that clause. Graduated sanctions, Ostrom's fifth design principle, are absent, and the halt is all or nothing.
- **Habermas and Goodin would challenge who counts as affected.** (U) and Goodin's all-affected principle (*Philosophy & Public Affairs* 35, 2007) include everyone affected. R8 binds only those who joined, and outsiders "bear nothing" (`outsider_bears_nothing`, Reciprocity.lean §6). That is the shift from all-affected to all-subjected that Fraser and Goodin argue over, and the README should name it.
- **Sentence 2 barely appears in Cascade.** `Ladder` (Cascade.lean:109–111) holds only "the holder is human" and "the hold grant is revocable", and the second repeats `Record.revocable` (Cascade.lean:121). Neither the reach from self to ecosystem nor the made/existing priority (`MadeExisting`, Reciprocity.lean:345) is in the file.
- **Exit needs a cost premise.** Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), is the standard citation for exit plus contest. Its central point is R7: exit disciplines only when leaving is cheap.

## 3. What to import directly, and what only to cite

### Import directly

1. **A real noninterference theorem.** Either prove the universal relabelling statement over all worlds, or type the rules over a projected `Facts` structure so they cannot read `role` or `inner`. The second makes §9's "type discipline" claim true by construction.
2. **A bridge lemma from Cascade's sentence 1 to §15's swap.** Define a map from acts to before-and-after outcomes and prove `lowersWeak a = true → passesSwap … = false` under stated assumptions about that map. Without it, call Cascade's sentence 1 a list of side constraints.
3. **Pareto, maximin and leximin side by side.** State all three next to `passesSwap`, with a short theorem for each case where they disagree, so the steward chooses. If the intended rule is "the weak position decides", leximin is the definition to adopt.
4. **ADICO tagging of the eleven promises.** Encode Attributes, Deontic, aIm, Conditions and Or-else for each. Missing sanction fields then appear mechanically, and institutional analysts can read the table directly.
5. **GSN vocabulary on `standsOn`.** Label each `X_*` as Assumption, Justification or Context, and call `bare` and `wideWith` defeaters. A safety assessor then reads the map as a safety case.
6. **A deontic representation for P7 and P8.** Follow LogiKEy's shallow-embedding pattern, or Governatori and Rotolo's defeasible deontic logic, so that contrary-to-duty remedies and lexical priority have a standard form.
7. **Delegation chains and attenuation in `Grant`.** Add them in the object-capability style and separate permission from authority as Miller does. Today an operate grant may come from a made grantor, because only hold grants require a human grantor (Cascade.lean:103, 110).

### Cite, do not copy

Rawls's positions and veil, Harsanyi, Kant, Hare, Habermas's (U), Hart's secondary rules, Kelsen, Suber's Nomic, Hirschman, Ostrom's design principles, Constitutional AI, and the blockchain governance specifications. These frame the claims but share no formal object with the model.

## 4. Three attacks a hostile reviewer would make first

The strongest hostile reader is a formally trained contractualist drawing on Scanlon, Rawls and social choice.

1. **"Your swap test is a status-quo Pareto veto."** `acceptsFrom .strong` blocks every rule that costs the strong seat, which protects the strong rather than giving decisive weight to the vulnerable. The README's "the weak position decides" overstates Reciprocity.lean:626, and Cascade never uses the test.
2. **"The derivations are definitional."** The prohibitions were written into `lowersWeak` and `Record'` and read back out. The witnesses `bare` and `wideWith` show only that a world can leave unset a flag no sentence mentions. The four-and-seven count measures where text was placed, not what follows. A real independence result needs a world that satisfies a natural, independently motivated reading of the basis, such as the outcome-based swap, in which a promise fails.
3. **"'Positions, not persons' does not hold."** `made` is an unexchangeable status doing the work kind used to do, and `isHuman := !made` reintroduces kind. Reciprocity §14 grounds stake and grievance in sentience, so "only the relationship is governed" holds in Cascade only by leaving fields unread while the companion file reads them. The halt rests on R1's stipulation, which §1'' concedes the symmetry is silent on, and the text should present it as a stated status rule, not as a yield of reciprocity.
